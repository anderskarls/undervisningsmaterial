# Cognitive Load Theory — Recent Developments 2024-2025

**Research Report**
**Compiled:** 2026-05-18
**Audience:** Swedish upper-secondary teacher (samhällskunskap & historia), Rosenshine-rooted practice
**Language:** English (insights to be translated to Swedish permanent notes)
**Scope:** 25 distinct papers/developments, focus on 2024-2025 with foundational anchors

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Cognitive Load Theory (CLT) is in the middle of its most substantial theoretical reorganization since the 1998 tripartite model. Seven headline shifts define the 2024-2025 landscape:

1. **The tripartite model is officially in retreat.** Sweller, Kalyuga and Paas have moved to a **two-factor model** (intrinsic + extraneous load only); "germane load" is now reframed as a proportion of intrinsic load rather than a third type. The 2025 Educational Psychology Review article *An Integrated Human Cognitive Architecture* (Sweller) formalizes this. Empirical work in 2025 (Memphis dissertation, Sweller & Sweller) confirms that questionnaire structures collapse cleanly when germane items are dropped.

2. **Element interactivity is the new core construct.** Rather than three load types, CLT increasingly explains learning effects through *element interactivity* — the number of elements that must be processed simultaneously to make sense of material. This unifies expertise reversal, worked-example, modality, and split-attention effects under one mechanism.

3. **The expertise reversal effect is now meta-analytically robust.** Tetzlaff et al. (2025), 60 studies, 5,924 participants: low-prior-knowledge learners benefit from high assistance (d = +0.51); high-prior-knowledge learners benefit from low assistance (d = -0.43). This is one of the most replicable effects in educational psychology and the empirical cornerstone of adaptive teaching.

4. **CLT and "desirable difficulties" have been formally reconciled.** Pyke, Lunau & Javadi (2024, *QJEP*) propose an integrated model where difficulty helps in *low* element-interactivity tasks (vocabulary, fact retrieval) and hurts in *high* element-interactivity tasks (complex problem solving). This dissolves the apparent contradiction between Bjork's framework and Sweller's.

5. **Generative AI is now CLT's most contested boundary.** Multiple 2024-2025 RCTs show ChatGPT-as-crutch reduces in-task load but produces **worse retention** (Lehmann et al. 2025: 57.5% vs 68.5% correct on a 45-day delayed test). Microsoft Research (Lee et al. 2025) and Gerlich (2025) document negative correlations between AI reliance and critical thinking, mediated by cognitive offloading. But *structured* AI use (Interactive Cognitive Offload framework) increases germane load and critical thinking — the design matters more than the tool.

6. **Embodied cognition is being integrated, not opposed.** Skulmowski (2025, *Nature Human Behaviour*) and meta-analytic work (17 studies, 1,046 participants, *Frontiers in Psychology* 2025) show embodied learning can *reduce* cognitive load when calibrated to task type — synergy, not conflict.

7. **CLT faces serious philosophical critique.** A 2024 paper in *Educational Philosophy and Theory* and a 2025 piece in *Brain Sciences* argue CLT's working-memory bottleneck model is neuroscientifically outdated and ignores social-emotional, sociocultural, and embodied dimensions. These are not fringe critiques — they appear in mainstream journals and frame CLT's individualistic cognitive frame as the next theoretical frontier.

**Bottom-line for a Swedish SO/historia teacher:** The Rosenshine-rooted practice you already use is more empirically supported than ever (especially worked examples, small steps, scaffolding for novices). But the 2024-2025 literature pushes you to *adapt* assistance to expertise (fade scaffolds explicitly), *integrate* generative work carefully (productive failure followed by instruction beats either alone), and *design* AI use as active interrogation rather than offloading. The "germane load" you may have learned about in teacher training has been retired by its creators — speak instead of intrinsic complexity and how to manage it.

## 2. State of CLT 2024-2025: What Shifted from the 1988-2020 Canon

### 2.1 The shift from three load types to two

The 1998 paper by Sweller, van Merriënboer & Paas introduced **intrinsic / extraneous / germane** load as the canonical tripartite model. By 2010 Sweller had already begun to argue germane load could be folded into intrinsic. Between 2020 and 2025, the field has split into two camps:

- **Two-factor camp** (Sweller, Kalyuga, Paas, current consensus): only intrinsic and extraneous load are needed. Germane resources are simply intrinsic resources directed productively.
- **Three-factor holdouts**: many researchers and instruments (Klepsch et al. 2017, Leppink et al. 2013) still measure three loads. But 2024-2025 psychometric work (the Memphis dissertation; Liu & Zhang 2025) consistently finds germane items load weakly and confound with the other two.

The *practical* implication, in Sweller's own words, is that "both models support exactly the same guidelines for the design of education and training." This is an internal theory revision; the classroom recommendations are stable.

### 2.2 Element interactivity becomes the explanatory mechanism

Rather than "is this load intrinsic or extraneous?", recent CLT explains effects through how many elements interact:
- **Expertise reversal**: experts have schemas that *chunk* interacting elements into single units, so assistance designed for novices becomes redundant.
- **Worked examples**: reduce element interactivity during initial encoding.
- **Split-attention**: artificially increases element interactivity by forcing learners to mentally integrate separated sources.

This unifies the dozen-plus "CLT effects" under one construct. Pyke et al. (2024) explicitly use element interactivity to reconcile CLT with the Desirable Difficulties Framework.

### 2.3 CLT now formally integrates motivation, emotion, and self-regulation

The 2025 special issue *Cognitive Load Theory: Emerging Trends and Innovations* (Education Sciences) contains papers on:
- mastery goal orientation × worked example effect (Education Sciences 14(6), 597, 2024)
- interest × mental effort (Education Sciences 14(10), 1044, 2024)
- cognitive reappraisal as a bridge between emotion and load (14(8), 870, 2024)
- self-regulated learning × cognitive load (Gorbunova et al. 2024, 14(8), 860)

This is a substantive theoretical expansion. CLT 1990s-2010s treated affect and motivation as external; CLT 2024-2025 treats them as resource-modulators that must be designed for.

### 2.4 CLT acknowledges replication failures and theory expansion

Sweller (2023, *Educational Psychology Review* 36, 10648-023-09817-2) explicitly frames recent CLT history as one of "replication crises and incorporation of other theories" leading to theory expansion. This is unusual candor from a major theory's originator. It signals the field has shifted from defense to integration.

## 3. Detailed Findings by Target Area

### Section 1 — CLT Framework Revisions (Two-Factor Model)

**Sweller, J. (2025). An Integrated Human Cognitive Architecture.** *Educational Psychology Review*, 37. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10089-1
This synthesis paper formalizes the integrated cognitive architecture (working memory + long-term memory + biologically primary mechanisms) and codifies the two-factor load model.

**Sweller, J. (2024). Cognitive load theory and individual differences.** *Learning and Individual Differences*, 110. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lindif.2024.102423
Argues that individual differences in learning outcomes are *primarily* determined by long-term memory contents (acquired schemas), not by raw working memory capacity. Implication: instructional design that builds schemas equalizes outcomes more than instruction that accommodates capacity differences.

**Memphis dissertation (2025). Rethinking Cognitive Load: Validating a Two-Factor Framework of Cognitive Load Across Problem-Solving Formats with Differing Levels of Guidance.** University of Memphis. https://digitalcommons.memphis.edu/etd/3837/
Empirical confirmation that factor analyses support a two-factor structure (intrinsic + extraneous) when germane items are removed from existing instruments.

**Critique (mainstream):** Some researchers still defend a three-factor model. The germane-load debate has practical relevance only for questionnaire design; the instructional implications are identical.

---

### Section 2 — Expertise Reversal Effect

**Tetzlaff, L., Simonsmeier, B., Peters, T., & Brod, G. (2025). A cornerstone of adaptivity – A meta-analysis of the expertise reversal effect.** *Learning and Instruction*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102166 (ScienceDirect S0959475225000660)
- **176 effect sizes from 60 experimental studies, 5,924 participants**
- Low-prior-knowledge learners + high-assistance instruction: **d = +0.505**
- High-prior-knowledge learners + low-assistance instruction: **d = -0.428**
- Effect robust across domain, age, instruction type
- Identifies expertise reversal as "a cornerstone of adaptivity"

**Practical implication:** Worked examples and detailed scaffolds are powerful for novices but actively harmful for students who already have schemas. The 2025 evidence makes the case for *fading* scaffolds nearly as strongly as for *providing* them.

**Curriculum design implementation (Foster, 2024).** *The Curriculum Journal*, https://doi.org/10.1002/curj.249
Designed a school mathematics curriculum that reduces worked examples as a unit progresses and replaces them with open, exploratory tasks — a concrete operationalization of expertise reversal.

---

### Section 3 — Worked Examples and Problem Completion

**Barbieri, C.A., Miller-Cotto, D., Clerjuste, S.N., & Chawla, K. (2023). A Meta-analysis of the Worked Examples Effect on Mathematics Performance.** *Educational Psychology Review*, 35(1). https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09745-1
- 43 articles, 55 studies, 181 effect sizes
- Mean effect of worked examples: **g = 0.48** (medium-to-large)
- **Counterintuitive finding**: adding self-explanation prompts *reduced* the worked-example effect (negative moderation). This contradicts a decade of conventional wisdom that pairing examples with self-explanation prompts is best practice.
- Correct examples > incorrect examples > correct + incorrect combined

**Anchor: Process vs. Product Worked Examples (2024).** *Education Sciences*, 14(8), 813. https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7102/14/8/813
Argues for *process* worked examples (showing reasoning steps) over *product* examples (showing solutions). Aligns with Rosenshine's modeling principle.

**The Worked-Example Effect × Mastery Goal Orientation (2024).** *Education Sciences*, 14(6), 597.
Worked examples primarily benefit learners with high mastery orientation. Prior knowledge, not example type alone, predicts transfer. Reinforces expertise reversal but adds a motivational moderator.

**Practical implication for humanities:** For history source analysis or argumentative essay structure, process-oriented worked examples (think-aloud modeling of how a historian sources and contextualizes a document) outperform end-product samples. Self-explanation prompts may be less universally helpful than commonly believed — use them selectively and verify they produce genuine elaboration, not surface compliance.

### Section 4 — Split-Attention, Modality, Redundancy Effects

**de Koning, B.B. (2024). Best of Both Worlds? Combining Physical and Mental Self-Management Strategies to Support Learning from Split-Attention Examples.** *Education Sciences*, 14(3), 284.
Combining annotation (physical) with mental connection-making reduced reported mental effort. Learning outcomes did not improve significantly — the effect was on *load reduction*, not necessarily transfer.

**Trypke et al. (2024). The More, the Better? Exploring the Effects of Modal and Codal Redundancy on Learning and Cognitive Load.** *Education Sciences*, 14(8), 872.
- **Modal redundancy** (image + text in same modality): facilitates learning, lowers cognitive load.
- **Codal redundancy** (spoken text + written text simultaneously): classic redundancy effect — increases load, hurts performance.
- Distinction matters: redundancy is not uniformly bad; the modality channel determines the effect.

**The Past, Present, and Future of the Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning (Mayer, 2024).** *Educational Psychology Review*. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09842-1
Mayer's reframing of his own theory now uses three load types — but maps them onto extraneous, intrinsic, and *generative* load (rather than germane). Generative load is "effortful active processing" — closer to Sweller's intrinsic-when-productively-used than to traditional germane.

**Lawson, A. & Mayer, R.E. (2024). Generative learning activities for online multimedia learning: when summarizing is effective but drawing is not.** *Frontiers in Psychology*. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1452385
- **Summarizing** during multimedia learning produced learning gains.
- **Drawing** did not, in animation-based lessons.
- Generative activities have boundary conditions — type of representation matters.

**Practical implication for SO/historia:** When using maps + text, prefer integrated layouts (labels on the map, not in a separate legend) — classic split-attention guidance still holds. When using video sources in class, written subtitles + narration = codal redundancy → cuts load *only* if learners cannot understand the spoken version alone; otherwise it harms. For source-analysis exercises, ask students to *summarize* a document in their own words (works) rather than always to *draw a concept map* (mixed evidence at best).

### Section 5 — CLT in Secondary Humanities and Social Science Teaching

This area is **underdeveloped** in the 2024-2025 literature compared to STEM. The most relevant pointers:

**Foster, C. (2024). Challenges in applying principles from cognitive science to the design of a school mathematics curriculum.** *The Curriculum Journal*. https://doi.org/10.1002/curj.249
Although math-focused, Foster's design principles (gradual fading, sequencing element interactivity, balancing examples with problem-solving) transfer directly to source-analysis sequences in history (sourcing → contextualization → corroboration → close reading).

**Denney, S.M. (2025). Controversial Public Issues Discussion: A Vehicle for Disciplinary Literacy in Civics.** *Literacy Research and Instruction*. https://doi.org/10.1177/23522798241294196
Discussion-based civics is effective but underexploits literacy goals. CLT framing suggests the *element interactivity* of multi-source civic issues is very high and must be sequenced (load one source's claims into long-term memory before introducing the next).

**BERA blog: How can cognitive load theory be applied to the teaching of history? (2023, still widely cited).** https://www.bera.ac.uk/blog/how-can-cognitive-load-theory-be-applied-to-the-teaching-of-history
Practical guide: pre-teach vocabulary; use timelines as schemas; sequence narrative before analysis; build period schemas before causal reasoning.

**Anchor (foundational): Wineburg, S.S. — historical thinking as "unnatural acts."**
Wineburg's framing of historical thinking as a counter-intuitive disciplinary skill (sourcing, contextualization, corroboration, close reading) aligns conceptually with biologically secondary knowledge in Sweller's evolutionary CLT: it requires explicit instruction because it is not naturally acquired. This bridge has not yet been formally developed in the literature — it's an opportunity for original synthesis.

**Practical implication:** The under-theorization of CLT in humanities is itself a finding. Apply STEM-derived principles to text-heavy teaching with caveats: source analysis is high in element interactivity, demanding worked-example sequences (think-aloud sourcing of one document before requiring multi-document comparison), gradual fading as students develop sourcing schemas, and careful management of contextual vocabulary load.

### Section 6 — CLT and AI-Assisted Learning

This is the area with the largest 2024-2025 empirical output, and the findings cluster into two opposing patterns:

**Negative findings — AI as cognitive crutch:**

**Lehmann et al. (2025). ChatGPT as a cognitive crutch: Evidence from a randomized controlled trial on knowledge retention.** *Computers in Human Behavior Reports*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chbr.2025.100691 (ScienceDirect S2590291125010186)
- RCT, 120 undergraduates
- ChatGPT users: **57.5%** correct on 45-day delayed retention test
- Traditional study group: **68.5%** correct
- Mechanism: reduced cognitive effort during encoding eliminated desirable difficulty

**Microsoft Research (Lee, M. et al., 2025). The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking: Self-Reported Reductions in Cognitive Effort.** Microsoft. https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/lee_2025_ai_critical_thinking_survey.pdf
Survey of 319 knowledge workers: higher confidence in AI tools predicts less critical thinking; lower confidence in AI predicts more critical thinking. The relationship is mediated by perceived cognitive effort.

**Gerlich, M. (2025). AI Tools in Society: Impacts on Cognitive Offloading and the Future of Critical Thinking.** *Societies*, 15(1), 6. https://doi.org/10.3390/soc15010006
Significant negative correlation between frequent AI tool use and critical thinking, mediated by increased cognitive offloading. Stronger effect among younger users.

**arXiv preprint 2507.00181 (2025). ChatGPT produces more "lazy" thinkers: Evidence of cognitive offloading.**
EEG study: participants using LLMs showed less brain connectivity than those writing without AI.

**Positive findings — structured AI as scaffolding:**

**Interactive Cognitive Offload (ICO) study (2025). Cognitive Offload Instruction with Generative AI: A Quasi-Experimental Study on Critical Thinking Gains in English Writing.** *Forum for Linguistic Studies*. https://doi.org/10.30564/fls.v7i.10072
- 240 participants, 12-week study
- Structured AI cycles (brainstorm → critique → revise) produced **significant improvements in critical thinking and essay quality**
- Mechanism: structured offloading frees working memory for higher-order evaluation; correlation between germane load and critical thinking gains

**Practical implication:** The same tool produces opposite effects depending on instructional design. Unstructured "ask ChatGPT" use causes offloading and retention loss. Structured use (prompt → critique AI output → revise) increases productive cognitive engagement. For history essays, an "AI critique protocol" (write a draft thesis, ask AI to challenge it, evaluate each challenge against sources) produces gains; "ask AI to write a draft I'll lightly edit" produces losses.

**Frontiers paradox paper (Jose et al., 2025). The cognitive paradox of AI in education: between enhancement and erosion.** *Frontiers in Psychology*, 16, 1550621. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1550621
Synthesizes the contradictory evidence as a fundamental design contradiction: AI both enables personalization and risks cognitive dependency. Cites the University of Pennsylvania study showing ChatGPT users answered 48% more problems correctly during practice but scored 17% lower on conceptual tests.

### Section 7 — Load Measurement Advances

**Liu, X. & Zhang, Y. (2025). Re-examining cognitive load measures in real-world learning: Evidence from both subjective and neurophysiological data.** *British Journal of Educational Psychology*, 95(2), 446-463. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12729
Combines subjective self-report scales (Leppink, Klepsch) with neurophysiological signals in a real-world classroom. Key finding: self-report and neurophysiological measures diverge for germane load, converge for intrinsic and extraneous. Direct empirical support for the two-factor model.

**Pei et al. (2025). Design and validation of an electroencephalogram-supported approach to tracking real-time cognitive load variations for adaptive video-based learning.** *British Journal of Educational Technology*. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.13535
Operational EEG-based real-time load monitoring; feasible for adaptive video learning.

**Pupillometry vs EEG (2024-2025 reviews).** *MDPI Brain Sciences* and others.
- Pupillometry: portable, wearable, detects load *intensity*.
- EEG: more invasive, detects load *type* and *location*.
- Feature-based ML on pupillometry alone now competes with EEG for cognitive load classification.

**Cognitive load scale for AI-assisted L2 writing (2025).** *Frontiers in Psychology*. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1666974
First validated scale specifically measuring cognitive load during AI-assisted writing — methodologically important as the domain explodes.

**Practical implication:** Subjective scales (Leppink, Klepsch) remain best for classroom use. Their germane subscale should be treated cautiously (likely confounded). For a teacher: a simple 1-9 mental effort rating after a task (Paas scale, 1992) remains the most validated single-item load measure and still works in 2025.

### Section 8 — Generative Learning and Desirable Difficulties Tension

**Pyke, W., Lunau, J., & Javadi, A.-H. (2024/2025). Does difficulty moderate learning? A comparative analysis of the desirable difficulties framework and cognitive load theory.** *Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology*, 78(10), 2181-2195. https://doi.org/10.1177/17470218241308143
The pivotal reconciliation paper. Proposes:
- **Low element-interactivity tasks** (word pairs, vocabulary, fact retrieval): increase difficulty (DDF wins).
- **High element-interactivity tasks** (complex problem solving, multi-source reasoning): reduce difficulty via worked examples (CLT wins).
- Integrates Perceptual Load Theory to explain attentional allocation.

**Investigating the Use of Productive Failure as a Design Paradigm for Learning Introductory Python Programming (arXiv 2411.11227, 2024).**
Empirical productive failure study: students who experienced productive failure *followed by* instruction showed the largest decline in cognitive load during the instruction phase and best learning outcomes.

**Kapur, M. (2024). Productive Failure: Unlocking Deeper Learning Through the Science of Failing.** Wiley/Jossey-Bass. ISBN: 9781394219995
Book-length treatment. Argues PF and CLT are complementary if sequenced correctly: a *first* phase of high cognitive load (PF) creates the conditions for a *second* phase of efficient schema consolidation (direct instruction).

**Practical implication:** Pair retrieval practice (low element interactivity tasks like vocabulary, key dates, definitions of *demokrati* / *välfärdsstat*) with explicit difficulty. For high element-interactivity tasks (analyzing a political party's program, comparing two historical sources), reduce difficulty via worked examples first, then increase. The framework dissolves the false choice between "make it harder" and "make it easier."

### Section 9 — Critiques of CLT

**Reyes, M. et al. (2025). Challenging Cognitive Load Theory: The Role of Educational Neuroscience and Holistic Learning.** *Brain Sciences*, 16(1), 109. https://doi.org/10.3390/brainsci16010109 (PMC11852728)
- Argues CLT's "fixed-capacity working memory bottleneck" is a simplification rejected by current neuroscience.
- Working memory is dynamically regulated, strategy-dependent, developmentally malleable.
- Proposes a Neurodevelopmental Informed Holistic Learning Framework integrating cognitive, emotional, interpersonal dimensions.

**Beyond the theoretical and pedagogical constraints of cognitive load theory (2024).** *Educational Philosophy and Theory*. https://doi.org/10.1080/00131857.2024.2441389
Philosophical critique: CLT's individualistic, content-focused frame ignores sociocultural learning, embodied cognition, ecological affordances. Argues CLT and inquiry-based learning are *not* genuine opposites; the dichotomy is methodological, not theoretical.

**Skulmowski, A. (2025). The synergy of embodied cognition and cognitive load theory for optimized learning.** *Nature Human Behaviour*. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-025-02152-2
Argues for integration, not opposition. Embodied learning activities can *reduce* cognitive load when they leverage biologically primary motor systems for biologically secondary content (Paas & Sweller 2012 framework).

**Embodied learning meta-analysis (2025).** *Frontiers in Psychology*. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1658797
17 studies, 1,046 participants. Embodied learning significantly improved academic performance and reduced cognitive load. **But:** effect is task-dependent and gender-moderated; not universal.

**Practical implication:** The strongest 2024-2025 critiques do not reject CLT but argue for *expansion*. CLT remains the dominant working theory of instructional design — even its critics use its constructs.

### Section 10 — Practical Classroom Guidance (What's Actually Changing)

Distilled from the 2024-2025 literature for SO/historia practice:

1. **Stop teaching three load types to colleagues; teach element interactivity.** It's the single explanatory mechanism the field is converging on.
2. **Make fading explicit.** Tetzlaff et al. (2025) make adaptive scaffolding empirically non-negotiable. Plan from day one when scaffolds will be removed.
3. **Worked examples for sourcing, contextualization, comparison.** Process-oriented (think-aloud reasoning) outperforms product-oriented (finished answers).
4. **Use self-explanation prompts cautiously.** Barbieri et al. (2023) show they can *hurt* the worked-example effect. Test whether your prompts generate elaboration or just compliance.
5. **AI use must be structured.** "Ask AI" produces offloading; "critique AI" produces germane processing. Build the critique protocol into every AI-assisted task.
6. **Sequence productive failure carefully.** A genuine attempt at a hard problem *before* instruction enhances later schema consolidation. Don't replace instruction with PF; pair them.
7. **Manage codal redundancy.** Don't read aloud while text is on screen unless students need redundancy (low expertise, language learners). Otherwise it adds load without benefit.
8. **Recognize that motivation and emotion modulate load.** The 2024 special issue formalizes what good teachers already know — anxious students have less working memory for content. Cognitive reappraisal interventions reduce extraneous load from negative emotion.
9. **Treat retrieval practice as low-interactivity training.** Use it for vocabulary, dates, key concepts. For high-interactivity skills (essay structure, source comparison), pair retrieval with reduced extraneous load via worked examples.
10. **Pre-teach vocabulary in text-heavy subjects.** Reduces intrinsic load during reading; aligns with the highest-frequency teacher recommendation in the 2024-2025 literature for humanities.

## 4. Cross-Cutting Themes and Emerging Debates

**Theme 1: From content focus to whole-learner focus.** The 2025 special issue's papers on motivation, interest, emotion, and self-regulation indicate CLT is absorbing what it once externalized. The intellectual move parallels what happened to Self-Determination Theory in the 1990s — a behavioural theory expanding into affect.

**Theme 2: The unit of analysis is shifting from "the lesson" to "the sequence."** Expertise reversal, adaptive fading, productive failure → instruction — all imply that what matters is not whether *this* task imposes high or low load, but how load is sequenced over a unit. This aligns naturally with Rosenshine's principles (which always emphasized weekly and monthly review) but pushes further toward macro-design.

**Theme 3: AI is the new "natural experiment" stress-testing CLT.** Three positions are emerging:
- AI as ultimate extraneous-load reducer (offloading → impaired retention)
- AI as scaffolder/tutor (well-designed reduces load productively)
- AI as forced productive failure (when AI is restricted, retrieval practice resurfaces)

The 2025 work is empirical chaos because the same tool produces opposite effects by design choice. Expect 2026-2027 to bring stronger theoretical syntheses.

**Theme 4: CLT vs. embodied/sociocultural framing is moving from opposition to layered integration.** Sweller (2024, 2025) explicitly cites Paas's biological-primary/secondary framework — which makes embodied cognition (motor system as primary) compatible with CLT (secondary content benefits from primary scaffolds).

**Theme 5: Measurement is catching up to theory.** Liu & Zhang (2025) and Pei et al. (2025) are pushing real-world neurophysiological measurement into educational research. The germane-load debate may be partly resolved empirically rather than theoretically.

**Open debate:** Is "germane load" dead? Sweller, Kalyuga, and the new instruments say yes. Mayer (2024) keeps it but redefines it as "generative load." Many measurement instruments still use it. Expect the term to fade from new research by 2027 but persist in textbooks.

## 5. Practical Implications for Swedish SO/Humaniora Classrooms

Specific, not generic — translated through the lens of samhällskunskap and historia teaching:

### History (historia)

**Source analysis sequencing.** Treat the four sourcing heuristics (sourcing, contextualization, corroboration, close reading) as four schemas to be built sequentially, not simultaneously. A novice student handed a primary source and asked to "analyze" it is being asked to perform all four schemas at once — guaranteed cognitive overload. Sequence:
1. **Worked example phase**: think-aloud sourcing of one document. Show how a historian asks "Who wrote this? When? Why?"
2. **Completion problem phase**: give a document with sourcing partly done; students complete.
3. **Independent phase**: students source unfamiliar documents.
4. Repeat the cycle for contextualization, then corroboration, then close reading.

This is Tetzlaff's expertise reversal applied to historical thinking, which Wineburg framed as biologically secondary knowledge.

**Pre-teaching as load management.** A text on Andra världskriget assumes schemas for *imperialism, nationalism, Versaillesfreden, depression, fascism*. Pre-teaching these (even 5-minute mini-lectures) before the main text dramatically reduces intrinsic load *for that reading*. Codal redundancy warning: don't read the text aloud while students follow visually unless reading skill is the bottleneck.

**Worked essay examples beat rubrics for novices.** Show a model essay with annotations explaining *why* each paragraph works — not just a rubric saying "include thesis, evidence, analysis." The annotated model is a process worked example; the rubric is a product description. Fade the model as students develop schemas.

### Civics (samhällskunskap)

**Mapping multi-source civic issues.** A controversial public issue (Denney 2025) requires students to hold party positions, ideological frameworks, empirical evidence, and personal values in working memory simultaneously. Element interactivity is enormous. Reduce by:
1. Building schemas for ideological positions first (separate from the issue).
2. Building schemas for the evidence base separately.
3. Then introducing the issue as a problem of *applying* known schemas.

**AI-assisted civic literacy.** Use the structured critique protocol: students draft a policy position → ask AI to steelman the opposing view → evaluate the steelman against evidence. This builds germane load while exploiting AI's capacity to generate balanced counterarguments. The Lehmann (2025) warning still applies: if students simply ask AI for "the right answer," retention drops.

**Discussion seminars as element-interactivity training.** A well-designed seminar increases interactivity productively (multiple sources of input must be integrated). Pre-loaded students (read the article beforehand, summarized key claims) have schemas ready; cold-called students with no preparation experience overload. Pre-reading is a load management strategy.

### Both subjects

**Exit tickets and Paas mental-effort scales.** A simple "rate this lesson's difficulty 1-9" is the most validated cognitive load measure for classroom use. Track over a unit to identify mismatches between intended and experienced load.

**The Rosenshine bridge.** All of Rosenshine's principles can be reframed through CLT 2024-2025:
- *Small steps* → manage element interactivity
- *Guided practice* → reduce extraneous load during early schema formation
- *Models* → worked examples
- *Check for understanding* → real-time load monitoring
- *Weekly and monthly review* → schema consolidation, fading

You don't need to choose between Rosenshine and CLT. CLT 2024-2025 is the cognitive theory *that explains why Rosenshine works*. Both are essentially saying: build schemas in long-term memory; adapt assistance to current schema state.

## 6. Open Questions and Contested Claims

1. **Is germane load truly dead?** Sweller/Kalyuga/Paas say yes. Mayer (2024) keeps a generative-load construct that functions similarly. Klepsch and Leppink scales still in wide use. **Likely outcome:** the term fades from new theory but persists in measurement and teacher training for 5-10 more years.

2. **Does AI tutoring help or hurt long-term learning?** Both 2025 RCTs and 2025 structured-AI studies are credible. The contested claim is whether the *average* student in the wild benefits or is harmed. Best evidence: depends on design discipline.

3. **How replicable are the classic CLT effects?** Sweller (2023) admits the field has had replication failures. Split-attention and worked-example effects are strongly meta-analytically supported. Other effects (transient information, isolated elements) have weaker support. The full landscape is not yet mapped.

4. **Does CLT generalize to humanities?** The literature is dominated by STEM examples. Theoretical extension is plausible (biologically secondary knowledge applies everywhere) but empirical work on history, civics, literature is sparse. Pyke et al. (2024) explicitly note vocabulary learning is well-studied but complex argumentation is not.

5. **Does the working-memory bottleneck framing match neuroscience?** Reyes et al. (2025) argue no — working memory is dynamic, not fixed. Sweller (2025) integrates this in *An Integrated Human Cognitive Architecture* but with more conservative claims. Theoretical resolution incomplete.

6. **How should social-emotional learning integrate with CLT?** The 2024 special issue starts this work but no unified framework yet exists.

7. **What is the role of generative AI in expertise reversal?** A novice using AI may experience less load than a novice without AI — but does this fast-track schema formation, or short-circuit it? The Lehmann (2025) RCT suggests the latter for the average case, but boundary conditions are unknown.

## Closing Note for Practitioner Use

The 2024-2025 CLT literature is unusually coherent for a research field: theory consolidation (two-factor model), strong meta-analytic evidence (expertise reversal), productive engagement with rival frameworks (DDF, embodied cognition, AI offloading), and serious philosophical critique from outside. A Swedish SO/historia teacher reading this in 2026 can reasonably treat the following as settled:

- *Worked examples for novices, fading for developing learners* — settled (Tetzlaff 2025).
- *Manage element interactivity, not three load types* — settled in theory (Sweller 2025).
- *Structured AI use beats unstructured AI use* — settled empirically (Lehmann 2025; ICO study 2025).
- *Pre-teaching vocabulary reduces load* — settled (multiple 2024 sources).

And the following as genuinely contested:

- *Whether germane load exists as a measurable third construct* — contested but the field is leaning toward "no."
- *Whether AI tutors will help or harm long-term learning on average* — open.
- *Whether CLT's working-memory model is neuroscientifically adequate* — open, with growing pressure for revision.
- *How to apply CLT to humanities-specific tasks (source analysis, argumentation)* — underdeveloped, opportunity for original pedagogical work.

The opportunity for a Swedish humanities teacher: the literature has STEM examples but the underlying constructs apply directly. Document your own classroom adaptations (history source-analysis sequences, civics multi-perspective design) — this is where original pedagogical knowledge has the highest marginal value in 2026.
