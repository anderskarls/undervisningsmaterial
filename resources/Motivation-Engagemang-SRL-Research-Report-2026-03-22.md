# Research Report: Student Motivation, Engagement, and Self-Regulated Learning in Social Studies/History Education (Upper Secondary)

**Date:** 2026-03-22
**Focus:** Ages 16-19, gymnasium level (SE) / upper secondary
**Coverage:** 2024-2026 research, with foundational frameworks
**Compiled by:** Research Specialist Agent (Cornelius)

---

## Executive Summary

The period 2024-2026 has seen significant advances in motivation and engagement research relevant to social studies and history education at upper secondary level. Three developments stand out: (1) a new generation of SDT meta-analyses with massive sample sizes (N > 388,000) that firmly establish effect sizes for need-supportive teaching; (2) a 2025 meta-analysis demonstrating the "specialized purpose" of different engagement types, with agentic engagement emerging as the strongest predictor of teacher support; and (3) the OECD Education Policy Outlook 2025 identifying early-to-mid adolescence as a critical inflection point where motivation either deepens or collapses. For a Swedish gymnasielarare in samhallskunskap/historia, the practical upshot is clear: autonomy-supportive teaching combined with explicit structure, formative assessment, relevance interventions, and explicit SRL instruction forms the evidence-based core of motivational design.

## 1. Self-Determination Theory (SDT) in Education

### Key Papers

**1a. Wang, Wang, Wang, Wind & Gill (2024) - SDT Interventions Meta-Analysis**
- *Source:* Learning and Motivation, Vol. 87, Article 102015
- *Sample:* 36 studies, N = 11,792 participants, 137 effect sizes
- *Key findings:*
  - SDT-based interventions improved intrinsic motivation, autonomy, and competence
  - Autonomy interventions: large effect in experimental designs (g = 1.14, p < 0.0001), smaller in pre-post (g = 0.19, p < 0.01)
  - Competence interventions: g = 0.48 (experimental), g = 0.58 (pre-post)
  - Interventions were effective across subjects and age groups
- *Implication:* Even brief autonomy-supportive interventions produce measurable gains

**1b. Howard, Slemp & Wang (2024/2025) - Need Support and Need Thwarting Meta-Analysis**
- *Source:* Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin (2025)
- *Sample:* 637 samples, N = 388,912, 8,693 correlations, 183 meta-analytic effects
- *Key findings:*
  - Need support and need thwarting are distinct constructs, not simple opposites (autonomy r = -.27; competence r = -.31; relatedness r = -.47)
  - Supportive behaviors correlated positively with performance, engagement, and well-being
  - Thwarting behaviors showed opposite patterns
  - Teachers who experience their own need satisfaction are perceived as more supportive
- *Implication:* Teachers must actively cultivate their own well-being to be motivating

**1c. Living Meta-Analysis Initiative (2025)**
- *Source:* Motivation and Emotion (2025)
- *Concept:* A continuously updated database of SDT findings (~30,000 effect sizes from 2000+ articles)
- *Key finding:* Prevalence of autonomy-supportive educational contexts varies by country and has changed over time
- *Implication:* The field is moving toward "living" syntheses that track how educational motivation changes across eras

### Core SDT Principles for Classroom Practice

| Need | Definition | Classroom Support Strategies |
|------|-----------|------------------------------|
| **Autonomy** | Sense of ownership and volition | Offer meaningful choices, use non-controlling language, provide rationale for tasks |
| **Competence** | Feeling effective and capable | Optimal challenge, specific feedback, scaffolding, visible progress |
| **Relatedness** | Belonging and connection | Warm teacher relationships, collaborative learning, caring classroom culture |

## 2. Interest Development Theory

### Key Papers

**2a. Hidi & Renninger (2025) - Four-Phase Model: Addressing Individual Differences**
- *Source:* Learning and Individual Differences (2025)
- *Key development:* Updated the classic four-phase model to address individual variation
- *Core argument:* Interest is universal (physiologically hardwired) but individually varying (genetics, personal characteristics, interactions, environment)
- *Phases:*
  1. **Triggered Situational Interest** - Attention caught by novelty, surprise, personal relevance
  2. **Maintained Situational Interest** - Engagement sustained by task meaningfulness and involvement
  3. **Emerging Individual Interest** - Student begins to voluntarily re-engage with content
  4. **Well-Developed Individual Interest** - Stable, self-generated curiosity and value

**2b. Need for Cognition and Interest Development (2025)**
- *Source:* PMC/Springer (2025), longitudinal study of 922 German secondary students
- *Key finding:* Need for cognition (NFC) predicts academic interest development (beta = 0.03-0.17), but NOT the reverse
- Both NFC and academic interest showed mean-level declines over 2.5 years
- *Implication:* Cultivating intellectual curiosity (NFC) is a precursor to developing subject-specific interest

**2c. Growth Cycle of Interest Framework (2024)**
- *Source:* Current Psychology (2024)
- A new model proposing interest develops through iterative growth cycles rather than strictly linear phases

### Practical Application for History/Samhallskunskap

| Phase | Teacher Strategy | Example |
|-------|-----------------|---------|
| Trigger | Use novelty, controversy, personal connection | "Should 16-year-olds vote?" debate |
| Maintain | Meaningful tasks with autonomy | Student-chosen research on a current political issue |
| Emerging | Encourage voluntary engagement | Optional current events discussions, student-led seminars |
| Well-developed | Support depth and expertise | Extended projects, mentoring, authentic civic participation |

## 3. Self-Regulated Learning (SRL)

### Key Papers

**3a. Zimmerman's Cyclical Model - Recent Extensions (2024-2025)**
- *Source:* Kitsantas et al. (2025), "Barry J. Zimmerman: Architect of Self-Regulated Learning"
- Zimmerman's three-phase model remains foundational:
  - **Forethought:** Task analysis, goal-setting, motivational beliefs
  - **Performance:** Self-monitoring, self-control strategies, metacognitive awareness
  - **Self-reflection:** Self-evaluation, causal attributions, adaptive reactions
- Recent extensions incorporate GenAI as SRL scaffolding (Huang 2024; Chang & Sun 2024)

**3b. Promoting SRL in the Classroom: Framework and Observation Study (2024)**
- *Source:* Metacognition and Learning (2024)
- *Key finding:* Teachers spent little time explicitly teaching SRL strategies, and when they did, cognitive strategies dominated while metacognitive strategies were rare
- Direct promotion (modeling) and indirect promotion (creating conditions) are both necessary

**3c. SRL Interventions Meta-Analysis (2024-2025)**
- *Source:* Multiple systematic reviews (ERIC, Wiley, Springer)
- *Key findings:*
  - Metacognition-based interventions more effective than pure cognitive interventions
  - Single-component, contextualized SRL programs with motivational support are most effective
  - Effect equivalent to approximately 7 months extra learning progress per year (EEF Toolkit estimate for metacognition)
  - SRL interventions are effective across ages but under-implemented in secondary schools

### SRL Teaching Protocol for Gymnasiet

1. **Explicit modeling:** Teacher thinks aloud about planning, monitoring, evaluating
2. **Structured practice:** Students use planning templates before tasks, monitoring checklists during, reflection protocols after
3. **Gradual release:** Move from teacher-directed to student-directed SRL over time
4. **Motivational framing:** Connect SRL skills to autonomy ("this gives you control over your learning")

## 4. Expectancy-Value Theory

### Key Framework (Eccles & Wigfield, updated)

Students' motivation is determined by two core beliefs:
- **Expectancy:** "Can I do this?" (self-efficacy for the task)
- **Value:** "Do I want to do this?" (subjective task value)

**Task Value Components:**

| Component | Definition | Example in Samhallskunskap |
|-----------|-----------|---------------------------|
| **Attainment Value** | Importance for identity | "Being an informed citizen matters to who I am" |
| **Intrinsic Value** | Enjoyment/interest | "I find political debates fascinating" |
| **Utility Value** | Usefulness for goals | "Understanding economics helps me manage money" |
| **Cost** | What must be given up | Effort cost, opportunity cost, emotional cost |

### Recent Research (2024-2025)

**4a. Growth Trajectories of Expectancy, Task Value, and Cost (2025)**
- *Source:* European Physical Education Review (2025), Shen, Li & Bo
- Students who sustained high expectancy and task value while reducing perceived effort cost showed best outcomes
- Cost is an independent predictor - reducing perceived cost is as important as increasing value

**4b. Expectancy-Value Profiles During Secondary-Tertiary Transition (2024)**
- *Source:* International Journal of STEM Education (2024)
- Students with "high expectancy/high value" profiles showed strongest engagement and achievement
- Profiles are malleable - can shift with targeted intervention

### Implication for History/Social Studies

The "relevance problem" in social studies can be reframed through EVT: students often have moderate expectancy but low utility value. Targeted utility-value interventions (see Section 9) can shift this profile.

## 5. Student Engagement: Four Dimensions

### Key Paper

**5a. Specialized Purpose of Each Type of Engagement: A Meta-Analysis (2025)**
- *Source:* Educational Psychology Review (2025)
- *Sample:* 652 effect sizes, 62 studies, 54 articles, N = 32,403 students (mean age 16.8)
- *Key findings (Fisher's z effect sizes):*

| Engagement Type | Strongest Predictor Of | Effect for Social Support (z) |
|----------------|----------------------|------|
| **Behavioral** | Achievement | 0.40 |
| **Emotional** | Motivation AND Well-being | 0.49 |
| **Agentic** | Teacher Support | 0.49 |
| **Cognitive** | No specialized function | 0.42 |

- Agentic engagement emerged as strongest predictor of teacher support, challenging prior assumptions
- Cognitive engagement operates as a "general" rather than specialized form
- Emotional engagement uniquely predicts both motivation and well-being

**5b. Classroom Social Climate and Engagement (2025)**
- *Source:* Humanities and Social Sciences Communications (2025)
- Teacher emotional support is foundational for mitigating boredom and disengagement
- Classroom climate is a necessary condition for engagement, not merely helpful

### Implications

Agentic engagement - where students proactively shape their learning (asking questions, requesting resources, offering input) - is particularly powerful because it creates a positive feedback loop with teacher support. Teachers who respond to student initiative become more autonomy-supportive, which further boosts engagement.

## 6. Motivation in Social Studies/History Specifically

### Key Findings

**6a. Factors Affecting Success in Samhallskunskap (Skolverket research)**
- *Source:* Skolverket article citing Hesby Mathe & Elstad (study of 264 Norwegian HS students)
- Two main factors drive effort in social studies:
  1. Students' perception that the subject matters for democratic citizenship and their ability to influence society
  2. Self-confidence in the subject
- Teaching that makes connections between effort and progress visible increases self-confidence

**6b. Skolinspektionen Quality Review: Samhallskunskap i Gymnasieskolan (2011, still relevant)**
- Vocational program students receive a shorter course and more factual instruction
- College-prep students get more analytical and theoretical depth
- This creates motivational inequity across tracks

**6c. Why Students Value History (Cairns & Garrard, 2024)**
- *Source:* Education 3-13 (2024), Australian students
- Students position the present and the future as key touchstones for measuring relevance
- The "so what?" question is central: students need to understand how historical knowledge applies NOW
- Character development and skills (argumentation, evidence analysis) seen as practical benefits

**6d. Active Learning in History (AHA)**
- Project-based learning and experiential approaches (simulations, service projects) increase motivation
- Students who "do history" (primary sources, investigations) rather than "learn about history" show higher engagement

**6e. Teaching Online Information Evaluation in Social Studies (2025)**
- *Source:* Theory & Research in Social Education, Vol. 53, No. 3
- As students encounter social/political topics online, teaching critical evaluation becomes a motivational as well as cognitive goal
- Teachers who frame source criticism as a personally relevant skill see higher engagement

### Subject-Specific Motivational Challenges

| Challenge | Evidence | Counter-Strategy |
|-----------|----------|-----------------|
| "When will I ever use this?" | Low utility value for history/civics | Utility-value interventions; connect to current events |
| Passive reception of facts | Lecture-dominated instruction reduces intrinsic motivation | Active learning, inquiry-based approaches |
| Abstract concepts | Political systems, economic models feel distant | Simulations, case studies, local examples |
| Track-based inequity | Vocational students get less rigorous/engaging content | Adapt content to program relevance without dumbing down |

## 7. Assessment and Motivation

### Key Papers

**7a. Formative Assessment and SDT (2025)**
- *Source:* Frontiers in Psychology (2025)
- Quasi-experimental study found formative assessment most strongly enhanced relatedness, potentially due to closer peer/teacher relationships
- Autonomy supported through self-assessment and goal-setting
- Competence fostered through constructive feedback and scaffolded tasks

**7b. Systematic Review: Formative Assessment, Motivation, and Achievement**
- *Source:* ResearchGate systematic review (2024 update)
- Formative assessment strategies showed positive influence on motivation, engagement, AND achievement
- FA bolsters metacognition and performance on summative assessments

**7c. Assessment Orientations and Motivation (2024-2025)**
- Grade-focused summative assessment builds maladaptive goal orientations (ego-threatening)
- Comments-only feedback produces stronger learning orientation than grades-plus-comments
- Self-assessment and reflection fulfill autonomy needs
- Limiting feedback, being specific in feed-forward, and praising effort over ability sustains motivation

### Practical Assessment Design for Motivation

| Practice | Motivational Mechanism | SDT Need Served |
|----------|----------------------|-----------------|
| Process-focused feedback | Shows growth, builds self-efficacy | Competence |
| Student self-assessment | Ownership of learning | Autonomy |
| Peer assessment with protocols | Social learning, belonging | Relatedness |
| Choice in assessment format | Personalized expression | Autonomy |
| Rubrics shared in advance | Clarity reduces anxiety/cost | Competence |
| Reflection journals | Metacognition, agency | Autonomy + Competence |

## 8. Autonomy-Supportive Teaching

### Key Papers

**8a. Patzak & Zhang (2025) - Blending Autonomy Support and Structure: Meta-Analysis**
- *Source:* Educational Psychology Review, Vol. 37 (2025)
- *Sample:* 94 studies, 110 effect sizes
- *Key finding:* Autonomy support and structure are independent but mutually reinforcing
- The field has been confused by contradictory definitions; this meta-analysis clarifies
- **Both** are needed simultaneously for optimal motivation - autonomy without structure leads to confusion; structure without autonomy leads to compliance without engagement

**8b. Autonomy-Supportive Intervention in Low-SES School (2025)**
- *Source:* Journal of Experimental Education (2025)
- Benefits of autonomy-supportive teaching are especially pronounced in low-SES settings
- Intervention training for teachers effectively increased autonomy support

**8c. Teacher Workshop RCT (2024)**
- *Source:* Teaching and Teacher Education (2024)
- 28 teachers (grades 7-9), 1,566 students, randomized
- Workshop participation increased autonomy-supportive teaching
- Key insight: learning autonomy support begins with perspective-taking - understanding what the student experiences

**8d. Patall (2024) - Agentic Engagement Pre-Print**
- *Source:* SDT Website Pre-Print
- Agentic engagement transcends passive motivation; it is proactive
- Students who show initiative foster reciprocal autonomy support from teachers

### Core Autonomy-Supportive Behaviors (Reeve)

1. **Take the student's perspective** before designing instruction
2. **Nurture inner motivational resources** (interests, preferences, competencies)
3. **Provide explanatory rationales** for tasks and rules
4. **Use non-controlling, informational language** ("you might consider..." vs. "you must...")
5. **Acknowledge negative feelings** when tasks are difficult or uninteresting
6. **Display patience** - allow time for self-directed learning

## 9. Relevance, Purpose, and Utility Value

### Key Papers

**9a. Hulleman's Utility-Value Intervention (foundational, applied 2024-2025)**
- *Core design:* Students write about how course content connects to their personal lives and goals
- *Effect:* Higher grades than control group (summarize-only), especially for at-risk students
- *Mechanism:* Increases perceived utility value, which boosts interest and performance
- Particularly effective for students with low initial confidence

**9b. OECD Education Policy Outlook 2025**
- *Source:* OECD (2025), draws on 230 policies across 35 education systems
- Identifies early-to-mid adolescence as a critical period where learners define who they are
- Key recommendation: "Environments that make learning relevant, relational and purposeful can sustain adolescents' motivation"
- Uses "Will-Skills-Means" framework: Will = motivation, socio-emotional well-being, curiosity, agency
- Dispositions like persistence, curiosity, and cooperation are connected with motivation and achievement

**9c. Purpose-Driven Learning**
- Students who connect academic work to a sense of broader purpose (helping others, contributing to society) show more sustained motivation than those driven purely by personal gain
- For samhallskunskap: framing democratic participation as meaningful purpose is a motivational lever

### Implementing Relevance in Samhallskunskap/Historia

**Utility-Value Writing Prompt Example:**
> "Beskriv hur det vi lart oss om [demokrati/ekonomi/rattssamhallet] kan vara relevant for dig personligen - i ditt vardagsliv, dina framtidsplaner, eller samhallet du lever i."

This simple intervention, repeated 3-4 times during a course, can measurably boost engagement and grades.

## 10. Motivation Decline in Adolescence

### Key Papers

**10a. UNSW Longitudinal Study (2024)**
- *Source:* UNSW Sydney (2024), published in phys.org
- *Sample:* 7,769 Year 6 students tracked through Year 9 (4 years)
- *Key findings:*
  - 18% total decline in motivation and engagement from Year 6 to Year 9
  - BUT: the link between teaching support and motivation strengthened each year
  - 40% increase in the role of teaching support between Year 6 and Year 9
  - Three critical support types: emotional, instrumental, management

**10b. Longitudinal Associations: Motivation and School Stressors (2025)**
- *Source:* Learning and Instruction (2025)
- Academic stress reduces motivation and achievement, increases dropout risk
- The transition to secondary school is a particularly vulnerable period

**10c. Decline of Academic Motivation: Psychological Need Satisfaction**
- *Source:* Garon-Carrier et al. (ResearchGate)
- Accelerated longitudinal cohort analysis confirms decline pattern
- Psychological need satisfaction partially buffers the decline
- Implication: SDT-based teaching becomes MORE important as students age, not less

### Counteracting the Decline: Evidence-Based Strategies

1. **Increase teacher support** - its impact grows stronger through adolescence
2. **Maintain autonomy support** - adolescents' need for autonomy intensifies
3. **Address relatedness** - peer connections and belonging become critical
4. **Reduce perceived cost** - manage stress, provide scaffolding
5. **Build competence beliefs** - process feedback, visible progress, mastery experiences

## 11. Teacher-Student Relationships and Motivation

### Key Papers

**11a. Systematic Review: Teacher-Student Relationships in Adolescent Motivation (2025)**
- *Source:* Educational Psychology Review (March 2025)
- Reviews conceptualizations, measurement, and analysis approaches
- Three key frameworks: Attachment Theory, MITB (Model for Interpersonal Teacher Behavior - Wubbels), SDT
- The MITB uses two axes: influence (controlling behavior) and proximity (social/academic approachability)

**11b. Effects on High-Tracking Students' Motivation (2025)**
- *Source:* European Journal of Psychology of Education (March 2025)
- Students perceiving high teacher-student proximity showed high academic motivation
- Emotional, behavioral, and cognitive engagement mediate the relationship between teacher-student relationships and achievement

**11c. Nurturing Bonds: Systematic Review (2025)**
- *Source:* Frontiers in Education (2025)
- Positive relationships defined by support, trust, and emotional care enhance engagement and achievement
- The dynamic interaction between teacher behaviors, student needs, and outcomes underscores the critical role of fostering relationships

**11d. Teacher Support and Learning Engagement (2025)**
- *Source:* Scientific Reports (2025)
- Teacher support significantly predicts secondary students' learning engagement
- Self-efficacy and academic emotions are key mediating variables

### Relational Strategies for Gymnasiet

- **Learn and use student names** consistently from day one
- **Show authentic interest** in students' lives outside school
- **Be available** for informal conversation before/after class
- **Acknowledge emotions** - validate frustration, anxiety, boredom
- **Maintain high expectations WITH high support** (authoritative, not authoritarian)
- **Use humor and warmth** without sacrificing structure

## 13. Emerging Trends and Paradigm Shifts

### 1. From "Motivation as Input" to "Motivation as Dynamic System"
The living meta-analysis initiative (2025) and profile-based research signal a shift from treating motivation as a stable trait to understanding it as a dynamic, contextual, multi-dimensional system that fluctuates within and across lessons.

### 2. Agentic Engagement as the "Missing Dimension"
The 2025 meta-analysis establishing agentic engagement as the strongest predictor of teacher support represents a paradigm shift. Students are not passive recipients of motivational conditions - they actively co-create them. This has major implications for pedagogy: teaching students to exercise agency (ask questions, request help, provide input) may be as important as improving instruction.

### 3. Productive Failure and Desirable Difficulties
Manu Kapur's "Productive Failure" (book 2024) consolidates evidence that initial struggle, when properly designed, produces deeper learning AND higher motivation than direct instruction. The mechanism: struggle activates prior knowledge, generates awareness of gaps, and makes subsequent instruction more meaningful.

### 4. Autonomy Support + Structure = Optimal Motivation
The Patzak & Zhang (2025) meta-analysis resolves a long-standing confusion: autonomy and structure are not opposites but complementary. The best classrooms provide BOTH clear expectations/scaffolding AND meaningful choice/voice. This contradicts simplistic "let students do what they want" interpretations of autonomy.

### 5. Cost as an Independent Motivational Variable
Recent EVT research emphasizes that reducing perceived cost (effort, stress, opportunity loss) is distinct from and equally important to increasing perceived value. Over-loaded curricula and high-stakes testing increase cost, which independently suppresses motivation regardless of interest or value.

### 6. AI and SRL
Emerging research (2024-2025) explores how GenAI tools can scaffold SRL phases - helping students plan, monitor, and reflect. This is a frontier area with both promise and risk for motivational autonomy.

### 7. Belonging as Foundation
The research consistently shows that relatedness/belonging is not a "nice to have" but a necessary precondition for other motivational processes. Without a sense of safety and connection, autonomy and competence interventions have limited effect.

## 14. Practical Recommendations for Gymnasielarare

### The Motivational Design Framework

Based on the research synthesis, here is an integrated framework for motivational classroom design in samhallskunskap/historia:

#### Before the Lesson (Forethought/Design)

1. **Plan for autonomy + structure simultaneously**
   - Offer 2-3 meaningful choices within a clear framework
   - Provide explicit learning goals AND allow student input on how to reach them
   - Design tasks with optimal challenge (not too easy, not overwhelming)

2. **Build in relevance**
   - Connect every unit to current events or students' lived experience
   - Use utility-value writing prompts 3-4 times per course
   - Frame samhallskunskap as a tool for agency: "This helps you influence your world"

3. **Plan for interest development**
   - Start units with triggering activities (controversy, surprise, personal connection)
   - Vary tasks to maintain situational interest
   - Provide depth opportunities for emerging individual interest

#### During the Lesson (Performance)

4. **Use autonomy-supportive language**
   - "You might consider..." instead of "You must..."
   - Provide rationales: "We're doing this because..."
   - Acknowledge when tasks are difficult or tedious

5. **Foster agentic engagement**
   - Explicitly invite student input: "What questions do you have? What would you like to explore further?"
   - Respond to student initiative with personalized support
   - Create space for students to shape discussions

6. **Scaffold SRL explicitly**
   - Model thinking aloud: "Before I start this analysis, I ask myself..."
   - Use planning templates, monitoring checklists, reflection protocols
   - Gradually release responsibility to students

7. **Leverage productive failure**
   - Present challenging problems before direct instruction
   - Allow struggle time (5-15 min) before scaffolding
   - Frame failure as information, not inadequacy

#### After the Lesson (Reflection)

8. **Formative assessment over grades**
   - Use process-focused feedback: what worked, what to try next
   - Build in self-assessment and peer assessment with protocols
   - Share rubrics in advance to reduce cost/anxiety

9. **Strengthen relationships**
   - Know students' names, interests, concerns
   - Show warmth while maintaining high expectations
   - Be emotionally available - acknowledge feelings

10. **Support belonging**
    - Create collaborative learning structures
    - Address exclusion and social dynamics actively
    - Ensure every student has voice in the classroom

### Quick-Reference: SDT Need Support in Samhallskunskap

| Need | Do This | Avoid This |
|------|---------|------------|
| **Autonomy** | Choice of topic within framework; student-led seminars; "why" before "what" | Controlling language; no choices; unexplained rules |
| **Competence** | Scaffolding; specific feedback; visible progress; optimal challenge | Vague feedback; tasks too easy or too hard; focus on grades only |
| **Relatedness** | Warm tone; collaborative work; know students as persons; acknowledge feelings | Distant/impersonal teaching; isolated individual work only; ignoring emotions |
