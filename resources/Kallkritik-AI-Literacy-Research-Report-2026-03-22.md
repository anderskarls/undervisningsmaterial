# Forskningsrapport: Kallkritik, mediekunskap, desinformation och AI-literacy i gymnasieutbildning

**Research Report: Source Criticism, Media Literacy, Disinformation & AI Literacy in Upper Secondary Education (Ages 16-19)**

**Datum:** 2026-03-22
**Forskningsfokus:** 2024-2026
**Syfte:** Evidensbaserat underlag for gymnasielarare i samhallskunskap

---

## Executive Summary

The field of source criticism education is undergoing a paradigm shift driven by three converging forces: (1) the failure of checklist-based approaches like CRAAP, (2) the explosion of AI-generated content including deepfakes, and (3) robust new evidence supporting lateral reading, inoculation/prebunking, and critical ignoring as teachable skills. Sweden's Skolinspektionen found in 2024 that 25 of 30 inspected upper secondary schools need to improve their source criticism instruction - particularly regarding digital and visual media. Meanwhile, the OECD/EC AI Literacy Framework (2025) and the upcoming PISA 2029 Media & AI Literacy assessment signal that AI literacy is becoming a core educational competency. For Swedish gymnasielarare, the most evidence-supported approach combines lateral reading instruction (Stanford/Wineburg), gamified inoculation (Bad News/GoViral), and explicit teaching about AI capabilities and limitations - moving beyond mechanical checklists toward authentic digital reasoning.

## 1. Lateral Reading & Civic Online Reasoning

### Key Research

**Wineburg & McGrew (2019) - Foundational Study**
Sam Wineburg and Sarah McGrew coined the term "lateral reading" based on research showing that professional fact-checkers, when encountering an unfamiliar website, immediately opened new tabs to verify the source's credibility externally - rather than reading vertically within the site itself. This contrasted sharply with both students and PhD historians, who spent time analyzing the site's own claims of authority.

- Source: Wineburg, S. & McGrew, S. (2019). Lateral Reading and the Nature of Expertise. *Teachers College Record*, 121(11).
- URL: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/016146811912101102

**Breakstone et al. (2021) - National Portrait of Student Failure**
The largest investigation of its kind administered assessments to 3,446 high school students with live internet access. Students struggled on all six constructed-response tasks evaluating online information. The findings revealed "alarming consistency" in poor performance from middle school through college.

- Source: Breakstone, J., Smith, M., Wineburg, S., et al. (2021). Students' Civic Online Reasoning: A National Portrait. *Educational Researcher*, 50(8).
- URL: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/0013189X211017495

**McGrew & Breakstone (2023) - Curriculum Efficacy RCT**
A randomized controlled trial tested COR materials across biology and geography classrooms. After completing six civic online reasoning lessons, high school students' scores improved from an average of 2.9 to 5.1 points (out of 14 possible). While statistically significant, the modest absolute gains suggest that more intensive, sustained instruction is needed.

- Source: McGrew, S. & Breakstone, J. (2023). Civic Online Reasoning Across the Curriculum. *AERA Open*, 9(2).
- URL: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/23328584231176451

**Digital Inquiry Group (DIG) - 2024 Spin-Off**
The Stanford team spun out as an independent nonprofit (Digital Inquiry Group) in January 2024, distributing over 160 free lessons. Partnerships with Los Angeles Unified and Chicago Public Schools are developing subject-specific COR materials for art, math, English, science, and social studies.

- URL: https://www.inquirygroup.org/

### Practical Implications
- Teach lateral reading as a *specific, named strategy* - students who learn it outperform those using intuitive evaluation
- Use the free COR curriculum (cor.stanford.edu) as a starting framework
- Integrate across subjects, not just social studies - the RCT shows this works in science and geography
- Expect modest gains from short interventions; sustained practice over time is essential

## 2. Inoculation Theory / Prebunking

### Key Research

**Signal Detection Theory Meta-Analysis (2025)**
A landmark meta-analysis re-analyzed 33 inoculation experiments (combined N = 37,025) using Signal Detection Theory within a hierarchical Bayesian framework. Key finding: both gamified and video-based interventions consistently improve discrimination between reliable and unreliable news *without increasing response bias* - meaning participants did not become blanket cynics. They increased their "hit rate" without causing more "false alarms."

- Source: Published in *Current Opinion in Psychology* (2025).
- URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352250X25002076

**Bad News Game & Feedback Exercises (2024)**
Research from 2024 found that adding feedback exercises after playing the Bad News game significantly boosts discernment compared with playing without feedback. The feedback mechanism creates an improved learning environment and enhances both accuracy and confidence.

- Source: Published in *PubMed* (2024). PMID: 39101907
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39101907/

**Bad Vaxx - Vaccine Misinformation (2025)**
Three preregistered RCTs (N = 2,326) showed the Bad Vaxx game significantly improved participants' ability to discern vaccine misinformation, confidence in doing so, and quality of sharing decisions.

- Source: *Scientific Reports* (2025).
- URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-09462-5

**Inoculation Videos Across 12 EU Nations (2025)**
Video inoculation materials were tested across 12 European Union countries, showing prebunking effectiveness across different cultural contexts.

- Source: *Communications Psychology* (2025).
- URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00379-3

**Biddlestone et al. (2025) - Six Prebunking Videos**
Development and validation of six inoculation videos that prebunk manipulation tactics and logical fallacies in misinformation, covering a range of techniques used in misleading content.

- Source: *Political Psychology* (2025).
- URL: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/pops.70015

**Important Caveat: Limited Real-World Transfer**
A 2025 study in *PNAS Nexus* found "limited effectiveness of psychological inoculation against misinformation in a social media feed" - while technique recognition improves in controlled settings, spontaneous detection in realistic scrolling contexts remains unclear.

- URL: https://academic.oup.com/pnasnexus/article/4/6/pgaf172/8151956

### Available Games for Classroom Use
| Game | Focus | Languages | URL |
|------|-------|-----------|-----|
| Bad News | General misinformation tactics | 20+ languages | getbadnews.com |
| Harmony Square | Political misinformation | Multiple | harmonysquare.game |
| GoViral! | Health/COVID misinformation | Multiple | goviralgame.com |
| Bad Vaxx | Vaccine misinformation | English | - |
| Cranky Uncle | Climate misinformation | Multiple | crankyuncle.com |

### Practical Implications
- Use inoculation games as *part of* a broader strategy, not standalone solutions
- Always include debriefing/feedback after gameplay - this significantly boosts learning
- Combine with lateral reading instruction for maximum effect
- Be aware that improved technique recognition does not automatically transfer to real social media contexts
- Address the overconfidence risk: students may feel more capable than they actually become

## 3. AI-Generated Content and Deepfakes

### Key Research

**UNESCO (2025) - Deepfakes and the Crisis of Knowing**
UNESCO warns that a projected 8 million deepfakes will be shared in 2025, up from 500,000 in 2023. Education must go "beyond detection" to teach students to navigate truth, knowledge, and AI-mediated uncertainty.

- URL: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/deepfakes-and-crisis-knowing

**Science Magazine (2025) - Deepfakes and Deep Learning**
Research published in *Science* explores the paradox of using deepfakes as an educational tool. Key finding: adults (including teachers and principals) exhibited *higher* vulnerability to deepfake science content compared to students, meaning educators may inadvertently teach misinformation. A 2024 Education Week survey found two-thirds of educators report students had been misled by deepfakes.

- URL: https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adr8354

**European Parliament (2025) - Children and Deepfakes**
A briefing on the specific risks deepfakes pose to children and adolescents, including non-consensual intimate imagery, cyberbullying, and erosion of trust in visual evidence.

- URL: https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/BRIE/2025/775855/EPRS_BRI(2025)775855_EN.pdf

**Detection Gap Research (2025)**
A national survey found that 33-50% of students, educators, and adults could not distinguish between authentic videos and deepfakes. There is a consistent gap between people's confidence in spotting deepfakes and their actual ability to do so.

- Source: Issues in Information Systems, Volume 26, Issue 4 (2025).
- URL: https://iacis.org/iis/2025/4_iis_2025_183-194.pdf

### Practical Implications
- Do NOT focus primarily on technical detection (it is a losing arms race)
- Instead, teach *provenance checking*: Where does this content come from? Who shared it? Can it be traced to a credible source?
- Use real examples of mislabeled/decontextualized photos (more common than sophisticated deepfakes)
- Address the emotional dimension: AI-generated content exploits emotional reactivity
- Teachers themselves need training - they may be more vulnerable than students

## 4. Swedish/Nordic Context

### Skolinspektionen Report (2024) - Critical Finding

Skolinspektionen conducted a quality review of source criticism education across 30 upper secondary schools. The results were concerning:

- **25 of 30 schools** need to improve their source criticism instruction
- Instruction rarely includes **images and video** - at more than one-third of schools, visual media is seldom covered
- Students describe instruction as **mechanical and checklist-based**, requesting more variation, closer connection to subject content, and relevance to their digital lives
- Teachers **rarely have adequate conditions** (time, training, resources) to develop their source criticism teaching
- Source criticism is taught primarily to prepare students for **further studies rather than civic life**
- The purpose of source criticism in instruction often seems disconnected from the digital reality students inhabit

- Source: Skolinspektionen (2024). *Undervisningen i kallkritiskt forhallningssatt - fokus gymnasieskolan*.
- URL: https://skolinspektionen.se/beslut-rapporter/publikationer/kvalitetsgranskning/2024/kallkritik
- Full report PDF: https://www.skolinspektionen.se/globalassets/02-beslut-rapporter-stat/granskningsrapporter/tkg/2024/kallkritik/undervisningen-i-kallkritiskt-forhallningssatt.pdf

### Swedish Government MIK Initiative (2024-2026)

The Swedish government commissioned Mediemyndigheten in March 2024 to implement a national initiative to strengthen media and information literacy (MIK) in the context of AI and disinformation:

- **Scope:** Broad, sustainable national effort targeting the general population
- **AI Focus:** Explicitly addresses how AI can be used in information flows and aims to strengthen societal resilience against disinformation
- **Collaboration:** Works through the MIK Sverige network (25 member organizations)
- **Timeline:** Renewed funding for 2025; final report due April 30, 2026
- **Web education:** A national web-based course launching autumn 2025

- URL: https://www.regeringen.se/regeringsuppdrag/2024/03/uppdrag-till-mediemyndigheten-att-genomfora-en-nationell-satsning-for-starkt-medie--och-informationskunnighet-i-en-tid-av-artificiell-intelligens-och-desinformation/

### Gy25 Reform (July 2025)

The Gy25 reform replacing course grades with subject grades took effect July 1, 2025. The new curriculum (Lgy25) explicitly states that schools shall:
- Develop students' **digital competence**
- Contribute to understanding how **digitalization affects individual and societal development**
- Give all students opportunity to develop **critical and responsible approaches to digital technology**
- Enable students to **evaluate information**

Samhallskunskap is restructured from seven courses into three subjects, with source criticism and digital competence integrated throughout.

- URL: https://www.skolverket.se/styrning-och-ansvar/forandringar-inom-skolomradet/gy25----amnesbetyg-pa-gymnasial-niva

### Nordic Collaboration

- **NORDIS** (Nordic Observatory for Digital Media and Information Disorder) develops media literacy campaigns and materials, led by Faktabaari with support from fact-checkers and researchers across the region.
- **Nordic MIL Index:** A feasibility study is underway for a standardized Nordic Media and Information Literacy Index across all five Nordic countries.
- **Finland** is widely considered the global leader in media literacy education, starting from early childhood through upper secondary. Sweden has focused on mapping MIK actors and connecting to UNESCO's policy framework.

- NORDIS URL: https://www.nordishub.eu/literacy/
- Nordic MIL overview: https://norden.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1534772/FULLTEXT01.pdf

### Nordic Classroom Study: Overconfidence in Fact-Checking (2025)

A recent Nordic study investigated a classroom intervention where upper-secondary students learned to verify war-related photographs (time and location). Key finding: while fact-checking strategies can be taught, some students developed *overconfidence* - believing they were effective fact-checkers without actually demonstrating the skills. This has important implications for metacognition in disinformation education.

- Source: Published in *Nordisk tidskrift for pedagogikk og kritikk* (2025).
- URL: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/20004508.2025.2558407

## 5. Digital Source Criticism Frameworks - Beyond CRAAP

### The Shift Away from Checklists

A 2020 working paper found that using the CRAAP method for information literacy education makes students "susceptible to misinformation" because it encourages evaluating a source *on its own terms* rather than checking what others say about it. Recent scholarship has critiqued checklist-style tools for promoting superficial assessments of information credibility.

### Current Best-Practice Frameworks

**SIFT Method (Caulfield, 2019)**
Four moves: Stop, Investigate the source, Find better coverage, Trace claims. Specifically designed for online evaluation. Advantages: faster than CRAAP, encourages lateral reading, works with the web rather than against it.

- URL: https://library.etbi.ie/sources2/sift

**Civic Online Reasoning (Stanford/DIG)**
Three core questions: Who is behind the information? What is the evidence? What do other sources say? Emphasizes lateral reading as the primary strategy.

- URL: https://cor.stanford.edu/

**Critical Ignoring (Kozyreva, Wineburg, Lewandowsky & Hertwig, 2023)**
A newer competence arguing that digital literacy must include *what to ignore*. Three strategies: self-nudging (removing temptations from digital environments), lateral reading (verifying credibility), and do-not-feed-the-trolls (recognizing attention-seeking content). Published in *Current Directions in Psychological Science*.

- URL: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/09637214221121570

**Kozyreva et al. (2024) - Comprehensive Toolbox**
Published in *Nature Human Behaviour*, this review of 81 papers classifies individual-level interventions into nine categories: accuracy prompts, debunking/rebuttals, friction, inoculation, lateral reading/verification, media-literacy tips, social norms, source-credibility labels, and warning/fact-checking labels. An accompanying interactive website lets educators explore the evidence.

- Source: Kozyreva, A., et al. (2024). Toolbox of individual-level interventions against online misinformation. *Nature Human Behaviour*, 8, 1044-1052.
- URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-01881-0
- Interactive toolbox: https://interventionstoolbox.mpib-berlin.mpg.de/

### Practical Implications
- **Replace CRAAP with SIFT or COR** as primary frameworks
- Teach **lateral reading** as the foundational skill
- Add **critical ignoring** as an explicit competence
- Use the Kozyreva toolbox as a reference for evidence-based intervention selection
- Move from "evaluate this source" assignments to "investigate this claim" tasks

## 6. Conspiracy Theories in the Classroom

### Key Research

**Jerome, Kisby & McKay (2024) - UK Teacher Survey**
A survey of 7,691 teachers in England found:
- **62%** of secondary teachers had encountered conspiracy theories from students
- **77%** agreed it was their responsibility to address them
- **49%** were worried about a student expressing conspiracy beliefs
- Teachers used a "wide range of contradictory strategies" including opening discussion, closing it down, challenging students, and reporting as safeguarding concerns
- Few teachers reported *successful* responses
- Several common strategies are likely to be **ineffective or backfire**

- Source: Jerome, L., Kisby, B. & McKay, S. (2024). Combatting conspiracies in the classroom. *British Educational Research Journal*.
- URL: https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/berj.3955

**UNESCO Teacher Guide (2024)**
UNESCO published practical guidance recommending two prebunking approaches:
1. **Logic-based inoculation:** Explaining misleading techniques and flawed reasoning in conspiracy theories
2. **Fact-based inoculation:** Communicating accurate information that counters false narratives

Key principle: Start education early, when children's trust in adults is still high. Media literacy and criticality should be embedded across subjects.

- URL: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/addressing-conspiracy-theories-through-education-unesco-guidance-teachers

**UCL Research Project (2025-2029)**
A major four-year research project running from April 2025 to September 2029 will develop a research-informed CPD program for teachers to respond to misinformation, disinformation, and conspiracy theories in the classroom.

- URL: https://www.ucl.ac.uk/ioe/departments-and-centres/curriculum-pedagogy-and-assessment/research/responding-misinformation-disinformation-and-conspiracy-theories-classroom

### What NOT to Do
- Do not dismiss or ridicule conspiracy beliefs (causes entrenchment)
- Do not engage in prolonged debate about specific conspiracy claims
- Do not assume reporting to safeguarding is sufficient
- Do not treat conspiracy beliefs as purely individual pathology

### What Works
- Teach the *techniques* of conspiracy thinking (pattern-seeking, cui bono fallacy, immune to falsification)
- Use inoculation approaches: expose students to weakened forms of manipulative arguments
- Build analytical thinking skills *before* conspiracy encounters
- Create a classroom culture where questioning is safe and valued

## 7. Student Vulnerability to Misinformation (Ages 16-19)

### Key Research

**Nature Human Behaviour Perspective (2025)**
A major review argues that adolescent-specific research on misinformation is urgently needed because:
- Adolescents undergo rapid **social, emotional, and cognitive changes** that affect susceptibility
- **Confirmation bias** may be particularly pronounced during identity formation
- Heightened **emotional reactivity** and sensitivity to social rewards can prioritize peer approval over accuracy
- Adolescents encounter different types of misinformation than adults (TikTok, Instagram, influencers vs. news media)
- The authors propose a Bayesian framework of belief updating tailored to social media contexts

- Source: *Nature Human Behaviour* (November 2025).
- URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02338-8

**Cross-Generational Misinformation Discernment**
A large-scale study with 66,242 participants across 24 countries found that **Generation Z demonstrated worse veracity discernment than older generations**. Additionally, younger adults were less accurate at distinguishing true from false news compared to older adults.

**Scoping Review: Young People and False Information (2025)**
A comprehensive scoping review in *Computers in Human Behavior* mapped responses to false information among young people. Key finding: higher education levels reduce susceptibility, and socio-demographics, cognitive biases, and peer dynamics all shape vulnerability.

- URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0747563225000974

**Development of Truth Discernment (2025)**
Research published in *Scientific Reports* demonstrates that media truth discernment and fake news detection develop in parallel with reasoning abilities during adolescence, suggesting interventions should align with cognitive developmental stages.

- URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-90427-z

**Leiden University Study (2025)**
Research from Leiden University explored how adolescents can become resilient to digital misinformation, finding that while they are vulnerable, they also possess developmental strengths (cognitive flexibility, social motivation) that can be leveraged through age-appropriate interventions.

- URL: https://www.universiteitleiden.nl/en/news/2025/11/how-adolescents-can-grow-stronger-in-a-digital-world-full-of-misinformation

### Age-Specific Risk Factors for 16-19 Year Olds
1. **Identity formation** - developing worldviews makes them susceptible to confirming information
2. **Peer influence** - misinformation from peers/influencers is more trusted than from news media
3. **Emotional reactivity** - fear, anger, and outrage in misinformation exploits adolescent emotional sensitivity
4. **Platform exposure** - heavy use of TikTok, Instagram, YouTube Shorts where misinformation spreads rapidly
5. **Overconfidence** - believing they are "digital natives" who naturally understand digital content

## 8. AI Literacy as New Educational Component

### Key Frameworks

**OECD/EC AI Literacy Framework (2025-2026)**
The most significant policy development: in May 2025, the OECD and European Commission released a review draft of "Empowering Learners for the Age of AI." Nearly 1,000 stakeholders contributed. Final version expected early 2026.

**Four Domains:**
1. **Engage with AI** - understand what AI is, how it works, its capabilities and limitations
2. **Create with AI** - use AI tools meaningfully and collaboratively
3. **Manage AI** - ethical use, privacy, data considerations
4. **Design AI** - understand how AI systems are built (for advanced learners)

The framework emphasizes critical thinking, ethics, and human judgment alongside technical understanding.

- URL: https://ailiteracyframework.org/
- Review draft: https://ailiteracyframework.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/AILitFramework_ReviewDraft.pdf

**PISA 2029 - Media & AI Literacy (MAIL)**
PISA will include a new "innovative domain" in 2029: Media and Artificial Intelligence Literacy. The first preliminary draft assessment framework was published in January 2026. This will assess how well 15-year-olds can engage with digital media and AI-mediated content effectively, ethically, and responsibly. Results expected December 2031.

- URL: https://www.oecd.org/en/about/projects/pisa-2029-media-and-artificial-intelligence-literacy.html

**World Economic Forum (2025)**
WEF published a major position paper arguing AI literacy is now a "core competency" in education, not an optional addition.

- URL: https://www.weforum.org/stories/2025/05/why-ai-literacy-is-now-a-core-competency-in-education/

**Springer - Integrating AI Literacy into Teacher Education (2025)**
A critical perspective paper in *Discover Artificial Intelligence* argues for integrating AI literacy into pre-service teacher education, noting that longer-term, multifaceted interventions combining conceptual knowledge, ethical reflection, and critical thinking are needed.

- URL: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44163-025-00475-7

### Key AI Literacy Topics for Gymnasiet
1. **How AI generates text/images/video** - basic understanding of models, training data, hallucinations
2. **AI output verification** - fact-checking AI-generated claims against reliable sources
3. **Bias in AI systems** - how training data reflects and amplifies societal biases
4. **AI and deepfakes** - understanding synthetic media creation and its implications
5. **Responsible AI use** - when and how to use AI tools ethically in schoolwork
6. **AI limitations** - confidently wrong outputs, lack of understanding, recency gaps

### The Metacognitive Laziness Risk
Research warns that "offloading cognitive work to chatbots can lead to metacognitive laziness, reducing engagement and long-term skill development." Successfully completing tasks with AI does not necessarily translate into learning. Strategies like limiting copy-paste functionality and requiring explicit verification steps are recommended.

- Source: Frontiers in Education (2025). Generative AI use in K-12 education: a systematic review.
- URL: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2025.1647573/full

## 9. Assessment of Source Criticism Skills

### Current Best Practices

**Performance-Based Assessment**
The COR program uses constructed-response tasks where students evaluate real websites with live internet access. This is far more valid than multiple-choice tests of media literacy knowledge. Students must demonstrate actual evaluation behavior, not just recall definitions.

**Measuring What Matters (2025)**
Harvard Kennedy School's Misinformation Review published research on "new types of assessments" for online source evaluation, arguing that traditional assessments fail to capture the dynamic, process-oriented nature of digital source criticism.

- URL: https://misinforeview.hks.harvard.edu/article/measuring-what-matters-investigating-what-new-types-of-assessments-reveal-about-students-online-source-evaluations/

**Signal Detection Theory Approach**
The 2025 meta-analysis of inoculation interventions demonstrates the value of measuring both *sensitivity* (ability to distinguish real from fake) and *response bias* (tendency toward general skepticism or credulity). This provides a more nuanced assessment than simple accuracy scores.

**The Overconfidence Problem**
The Nordic study on war photo verification (2025) found that some students became overconfident after instruction - reporting higher confidence without corresponding accuracy gains. This suggests assessments must include calibration measures (comparing confidence with actual performance).

### Recommended Assessment Approaches
1. **Live internet tasks** - give students a claim or website and ask them to evaluate it with real tools
2. **Process documentation** - assess the *strategies* students use (lateral reading, source investigation), not just their conclusions
3. **Before/after measures** - pre- and post-test to show growth
4. **Confidence calibration** - pair accuracy measures with self-reported confidence
5. **Transfer tasks** - test whether skills apply to novel contexts, not just practiced scenarios

## 10. Teacher Preparedness

### Key Findings

**The Preparation Gap**
A 2020 Media Literacy Now survey found that while **80% of teachers agreed media literacy is essential**, only **20% felt adequately prepared** to teach it. Time constraints and lack of standardized curricula further hinder their efforts.

**Swedish Context (Skolinspektionen, 2024)**
The Swedish School Inspectorate found that teachers at gymnasieskolor rarely have the prerequisites to develop their source criticism instruction. This includes insufficient time, limited access to current materials, and inadequate professional development.

**Teachers' Own Digital Vulnerabilities**
A 2025 Frontiers in Education study reveals "significant gaps between teachers' digital engagement patterns and their critical evaluation skills, particularly in visual content assessment." The Science deepfakes study found that teachers and principals were *more* vulnerable to deepfake science content than students.

**Pre-Service Teacher MIL (2024)**
A systematic review of media and information literacy among pre-service teachers (2013-2024) published in ScienceDirect identifies key gaps in teacher preparation programs, noting that MIL is gaining academic attention but practical preparation remains insufficient.

- URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2667096825000308

**Fostering AI Literacy in Pre-Service Teachers (2025)**
Research from Frontiers in Education found that targeted training interventions can improve pre-service teachers' awareness, attitude, and trust in AI, but the effects require sustained, multi-faceted approaches combining conceptual knowledge, ethical reflection, and critical thinking.

- URL: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2025.1668078/full

### Practical Implications for Professional Development
- Teachers need training in *the same strategies* they will teach (lateral reading, SIFT, inoculation)
- Professional development should include hands-on practice with current AI tools
- Schools should allocate dedicated time for collaborative source criticism curriculum development
- Connect to the Mediemyndigheten web education (launching autumn 2025)

## Emerging Trends & Synthesis

### 1. From Detection to Navigation
The field is shifting from "can you spot the fake?" to "how do you navigate an uncertain information landscape?" This reflects the reality that detection tools will always lag behind generation tools.

### 2. AI Literacy as Mandatory
With PISA 2029 MAIL and the OECD/EC framework, AI literacy is becoming a measured, standardized educational outcome. Sweden should prepare for this now.

### 3. Convergence of Interventions
The Kozyreva et al. (2024) toolbox shows that no single intervention is sufficient. The most effective approach combines multiple strategies: inoculation + lateral reading + accuracy prompts + friction.

### 4. Metacognition as Key
Both the Nordic overconfidence study and the adolescent vulnerability research point to metacognition - knowing what you know and do not know - as the critical underlying skill. Source criticism education must teach self-awareness alongside strategies.

### 5. The Visual Turn
Skolinspektionen's finding that Swedish schools neglect visual media is a critical gap. Students encounter misinformation primarily through images, short videos, and social media formats - not the text-based sources schools typically teach with.

### 6. Nordic Leadership Opportunity
Finland's model and the emerging Nordic MIL Index, combined with the Mediemyndigheten initiative, position Sweden to be a leader in this space. But the Skolinspektionen report shows significant work is needed at the school level.

## Recommendations for Swedish Gymnasielarare i Samhallskunskap

### Immediate Actions
1. **Replace CRAAP checklists** with SIFT method or COR framework
2. **Teach lateral reading explicitly** - name it, model it, practice it repeatedly
3. **Use inoculation games** (Bad News, Harmony Square) with structured debriefing
4. **Include visual media** in source criticism exercises (reverse image search, provenance checking)
5. **Address AI directly** - show students how ChatGPT can generate convincing but false content

### Medium-Term Development
6. **Build AI literacy modules** aligned with the OECD/EC four-domain framework
7. **Create assessment tasks** using live internet evaluation (not worksheets)
8. **Connect to Mediemyndigheten resources** as they launch in 2025-2026
9. **Develop cross-subject collaboration** (as COR research supports)
10. **Address conspiracy theories proactively** using inoculation rather than reactive debunking
