# Feedback for Learning: Recent Research Report (2024-2025)

**Compiled:** 2026-05-06
**Target audience:** Swedish gymnasielärare (samhällskunskap & historia)
**Recency requirement:** 80%+ from 2024-2025; 2023 only for foundational/highly-cited
**Coverage exclusions:** Basic formative assessment definitions, Hattie 2007 classic, generic retrieval practice, generic AI tutoring overviews

---

## Executive Summary

Feedback research in 2024-2025 has shifted decisively from "what teachers say" to "what students do with what they hear." The most generative recent agendas are: (1) **student feedback literacy** as a measurable, trainable disposition; (2) **internal feedback** generated by comparison processes (Nicol's program); (3) **GenAI feedback** which performs comparably to human feedback on most quality dimensions but reshapes engagement, trust and equity in non-trivial ways; and (4) **affect/identity** as the central bottleneck - "unheard and unused" rejection rather than information gaps now dominate the explanatory frame. Two contrarian findings stand out: (a) growth-mindset effects on achievement are essentially null in high-quality studies, undermining a common rationale for "you can grow" feedback framings; (b) immediate vs. delayed feedback is a context-dependent rather than universal trade-off, with classroom evidence often favoring immediate feedback even though lab evidence favors delayed.

For a samhällskunskap/historia teacher, the highest-leverage 2024-2025 findings are: source-based argument writing benefits from cognitive-strategy interventions tied to formative feedback cycles (Steiss et al. 2024; Olson et al. 2025); video feedback yields ~89% successful revision rates vs. ~72% for written; and dialogic/feedback-partnership designs reliably outperform monologue feedback for engagement.

## Topic 1: Feedback Literacy

### 1.1 "Can students' feedback literacy be improved? A scoping review of interventions"
- **Authors:** Little, T., Dawson, P., Boud, D., Tai, J.
- **Date:** 2023 (foundational review still actively cited 2024-2025)
- **Venue:** Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education
- **DOI:** 10.1080/02602938.2023.2177613
- **URL:** https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02602938.2023.2177613
- **Key findings:** Educational interventions can enhance feedback literacy (managing perceptions, building agency), but both improved study design AND intervention design are required. Many interventions are atheoretical.
- **Novel contribution:** First systematic scoping of intervention evidence, identifies the gap that 2024-2025 work tries to fill.
- **Classroom implication:** Don't expect feedback literacy to develop spontaneously - design explicit scaffolds.

### 1.2 "Highly informative feedback using learning analytics: how feedback literacy moderates student perceptions of feedback"
- **Authors:** Lim, L.-A. et al.
- **Date:** 2025
- **Venue:** International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education
- **DOI:** 10.1186/s41239-025-00539-9
- **URL:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41239-025-00539-9
- **Key findings:** Feedback literacy moderates how students perceive and act on the same feedback; high-literacy students extract more actionable information from identical inputs. Few studies had previously quantified this moderation.
- **Novel contribution:** Quantitative moderation analysis in a tech-supported context - shows literacy is a *gain amplifier*, not just a precursor.
- **Classroom implication:** Investing in feedback literacy training has compounding returns - same teacher effort yields more learning per unit of feedback.

### 1.3 "Students' feedback literacy in higher education: an initial scale validation study"
- **Authors:** Dawson, P. et al.
- **Date:** 2023 (highly cited foundational scale, still primary measurement instrument 2024-2025)
- **Venue:** Teaching in Higher Education
- **DOI:** 10.1080/13562517.2023.2263838
- **URL:** https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13562517.2023.2263838
- **Key findings:** Validated multi-factor scale operationalizing the Carless-Boud four components.
- **Classroom implication:** Diagnostic tool for identifying which feedback-literacy component is weakest in a class.

### 1.4 "Teacher feedback literacy as an emerging field: a scoping review"
- **Date:** 2026 (online first late 2025)
- **Venue:** Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education
- **DOI:** 10.1080/02602938.2026.2631533
- **URL:** https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02602938.2026.2631533
- **Key findings:** Teacher feedback literacy (TFL) is now recognized as a parallel construct to student feedback literacy. The teacher must also have skills to design, deliver, calibrate, and follow up feedback.
- **Novel contribution:** Maps the new field; argues that asymmetry in literacy between teacher and student is a primary failure mode.
- **Classroom implication:** **[NORDIC]** Useful frame for fortbildning - "we both need feedback literacy" rather than "students must learn to receive."

### 1.5 "Development and validation of a generic self-assessment scale for K-12 teachers as feedback givers"
- **Date:** 2025
- **Venue:** Humanities and Social Sciences Communications (Nature)
- **DOI:** 10.1057/s41599-025-04927-4
- **URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-04927-4
- **Key findings:** Validated scale for K-12 teachers across four components: knowledge, skills, values, actionability.
- **Empirical metrics:** Item response theory + factor analysis; usable in Swedish gymnasium context.
- **Classroom implication:** **[NORDIC]** Self-diagnostic tool - one of the few feedback literacy instruments built specifically for K-12 (not HE).

## Topic 2: Internal vs. External Feedback (Nicol's program)

### 2.1 "Do students generate better self-feedback by comparing their work against assessment criteria or exemplars?"
- **Authors:** Nicol, D. & Rose, A.
- **Date:** 2025/2026 (online 2025)
- **Venue:** Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education
- **DOI:** 10.1080/02602938.2026.2644513
- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2026.2644513
- **Key findings:** Comparing work against **exemplars** generates richer, more actionable internal feedback than comparing against criteria alone. Sequential comparisons compound the effect.
- **Novel contribution:** Direct experimental comparison of comparator types, settling a long-standing design question.
- **Classroom implication:** For history essays - show 1-2 exemplar paragraphs *before* students self-assess, not just a rubric.

### 2.2 "Promoting Learner Self-Regulation: Is It Better to Give Students Exemplars before or after Producing Work?"
- **Authors:** Nicol, D. & Rose, A.
- **Date:** 2025
- **Venue:** Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education 50(8): 1311-1331
- **Key findings:** Exemplars given **after** production with explicit comparison instruction yielded better self-regulation than before-production exemplars. Surprises the conventional "model the target first" assumption.
- **Classroom implication:** **Counterintuitive** - first draft, *then* exemplar comparison may beat exemplar-first.

### 2.3 "Shifting feedback agency to students by having them write their own feedback comments"
- **Authors:** Nicol, D. & Kushwah, L.
- **Date:** 2024
- **Venue:** Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education
- **DOI:** 10.1080/02602938.2023.2265080
- **URL:** https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02602938.2023.2265080
- **Key findings:** Having students write their *own* feedback comments increased agency and quality; reduced teacher workload. Students generated comments comparable to teacher comments in many cases.
- **Empirical metrics:** Qualitative analysis of comment quality; student survey data on agency.
- **Classroom implication:** Pedagogically and logistically attractive - solves the "marking pile" problem while building feedback literacy.

### 2.4 "Self-generated feedback with digital comparators and AI support to enhance feedback skills"
- **Date:** 2025
- **Venue:** ResearchGate preprint, mixed pilot study
- **URL:** https://www.researchgate.net/publication/396930617
- **Key findings:** AI tools serve as additional comparators; students extract internal feedback by comparing their work against AI-generated alternatives.
- **Novel contribution:** Hybrid AI+comparator design that bridges Nicol's framework into the GenAI era.

## Topic 3: Feedback Uptake / The Feedback Gap

### 3.1 "Unheard and unused: why students reject teacher and peer feedback"
- **Authors:** Lipnevich, A.A. et al.
- **Date:** May 21, 2025
- **Venue:** Frontiers in Education
- **DOI:** 10.3389/feduc.2025.1567704
- **URL:** https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2025.1567704/full
- **Key findings:** Five mechanisms drive rejection: (1) negative emotional reactions, (2) academic-language opacity, (3) lack of relevance/specificity, (4) felt powerlessness, (5) social/contextual relationship dynamics.
- **Novel contribution:** Reframes the "feedback gap" from cognitive (didn't understand) to **affective + relational** (didn't feel safe enough or empowered to act).
- **Classroom implication:** **High-leverage for gymnasium**: relationship quality and felt power may matter more than feedback comment quality.

### 3.2 "Promoting Innovative Learning-oriented Feedback Practice: Engagement, Challenges, and Solutions"
- **Date:** 2025
- **Venue:** Frontiers in Psychology
- **URL:** https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1707902/abstract
- **Key findings:** Disengagement is exacerbated by structural constraints (large classes, time poverty), not just individual disposition.
- **Classroom implication:** **[NORDIC]** Aligns with Skolverket emphasis on systemic conditions for formativ bedömning.

### 3.3 "Feedback partnerships: strengthening students' proactive recipience through co-creating dialogic feedback"
- **Authors:** [conceptual paper, Winstone-tradition]
- **Date:** Online January 27, 2025 (accepted Dec 16, 2024)
- **Venue:** Higher Education Research & Development
- **DOI:** 10.1080/07294360.2024.2445570
- **URL:** https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/07294360.2024.2445570
- **Key findings:** Six characteristics of feedback-partnership dialogue: (1) student-initiated, (2) adaptive to needs, (3) goal-aligned, (4) non-dominating, (5) reciprocal, (6) cognitively engaging.
- **Classroom implication:** Operational checklist for designing feedback conferences.

### 3.4 "Feedback-seeking behaviour as a self-regulation strategy in higher education: a pedagogical approach"
- **Date:** 2025
- **Venue:** Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education
- **DOI:** 10.1080/02602938.2025.2476621
- **URL:** https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02602938.2025.2476621
- **Key findings:** Feedback-seeking is a learnable self-regulation strategy. Pedagogical scaffolding (modelling, prompts, low-stakes seeking opportunities) increases student initiation.
- **Classroom implication:** Don't wait for students to ask - make seeking a routine through scheduled "feedback request" prompts.

## Topic 4: Feedback Levels (Hattie & Timperley updated)

### 4.1 "An Exploration of Feedback Using Hattie and Timperley's Feedback Levels"
- **Authors:** Lee et al.
- **Date:** 2025 (presented Ottawa 2024)
- **Venue:** Family Medicine (medical education)
- **PubMed:** 40526854
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12306611/
- **Key findings:** First three levels (task, process, self-regulation) remain most beneficial. Self-level feedback ("you're so smart") consistently underperforms. Empirical work on which transitions between levels are productive.
- **Classroom implication:** **High-leverage**: shift comments from task ("wrong date") to process ("when you mix chronology with thematic argument, signpost with a transition") and self-regulation ("how could you check this next time?").

### 4.2 "Feedback levels and their interaction with the mathematical reasoning process"
- **Authors:** Smit, R. et al.
- **Date:** 2024
- **Venue:** The Curriculum Journal (BERA)
- **DOI:** 10.1002/curj.221
- **URL:** https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/curj.221
- **Key findings:** Optimal feedback level depends on where the student is in the reasoning process - task feedback during early exploration, process feedback during execution, self-regulation feedback during review. **Time-of-task matters as much as feedback content.**
- **Classroom implication:** Don't apply "process feedback is best" universally - calibrate to phase of student work.

### 4.3 "How to optimise the use of Hattie and Timperley's feedback levels for student learning" (BERA blog)
- **Date:** 2024-2025
- **URL:** https://www.bera.ac.uk/blog/how-to-optimise-the-use-of-hattie-and-timperleys-feedback-levels-for-student-learning
- **Key findings:** Practitioner-oriented synthesis emphasizing the "matching" problem - high-information feedback is wasted on low-feedback-literacy students.

## Topic 5: Peer Feedback

### 5.1 "Enhancing the Peer-Feedback Process Through Instructional Support: A Meta-Analysis"
- **Date:** 2025
- **Venue:** Educational Psychology Review
- **DOI:** 10.1007/s10648-025-10017-3
- **URL:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-025-10017-3
- **Sample:** 32 studies, 3,806 learners
- **Key findings:** Instructional support has substantial effect on peer-feedback outcomes (g = 0.47). Feedback-provision support has even larger effect on quality of feedback given (g = 0.72).
- **Novel contribution:** Best evidence yet on the *training* component - peer feedback without training is mostly noise; with training it's powerful.
- **Classroom implication:** **High-leverage**: short calibration session + rubric + worked examples > unsupported peer review.

### 5.2 "Peer feedback in higher education: student perceptions of peer review and strategies for learning enhancement"
- **Date:** 2025
- **Venue:** European Journal of Higher Education
- **DOI:** 10.1080/21568235.2025.2457466
- **URL:** https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/21568235.2025.2457466
- **Key findings:** Peer review is valued as dialogic feedback practice; develops critical thinking and self-reflection. Students value *giving* feedback as much as receiving it.

### 5.3 "Effects of teacher, peer and self-feedback on student improvement in online assessment: the role of individuals' presumptions and feedback literacy"
- **Date:** 2025
- **Venue:** Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education
- **DOI:** 10.1080/02602938.2025.2530452
- **URL:** https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02602938.2025.2530452
- **Key findings:** Teacher, peer, and self-feedback all improve performance, but moderated by feedback literacy and prior beliefs about feedback source. Peer feedback effects strengthen when students believe peers can provide useful input.
- **Classroom implication:** Pre-empt "peer feedback isn't real feedback" beliefs explicitly.

### 5.4 "Promoting formative self-assessment through peer assessment: peer work quality"
- **Date:** 2025
- **Venue:** International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education
- **URL:** https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s41239-025-00522-4.pdf
- **Key findings:** Quality of *peers' work* (which students review) shapes the internal feedback the reviewer generates. Comparing across high-quality and low-quality samples helps reviewers calibrate own work.

## Topic 6: Corrective/Negative Feedback, Affect, Mindset

### 6.1 "Do growth mindset interventions impact students' academic achievement? A systematic review and meta-analysis"
- **Authors:** Macnamara, B.N. & Burgoyne, A.P.
- **Date:** 2023 (still primary critical reference 2024-2025)
- **Venue:** Psychological Bulletin
- **PubMed:** 36326645
- **Key findings:** When examining highest-quality evidence (6 studies, N = 13,571), effect was nonsignificant: **d = 0.02, 95% CI = [-0.06, 0.10]**. Strongest studies show effects ranging d = -0.01 to +0.065.
- **Novel contribution:** Major counter-evidence to popular growth-mindset claims; shows publication bias and conflict-of-interest patterns.
- **Classroom implication:** **CONTRARIAN** - Don't build feedback strategy on "fixed vs. growth mindset" framing; the empirical foundation is weak.

### 6.2 "Can growth mindset interventions improve academic achievement? A structured review of the existing evidence"
- **Authors:** Gazmuri et al.
- **Date:** 2025
- **Venue:** Review of Education (BERA)
- **DOI:** 10.1002/rev3.70066
- **URL:** https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/rev3.70066
- **Key findings:** Confirms 2023 critique with newer evidence; modest heterogeneity in effects with no robust moderators identified.

### 6.3 "Why Meta-Analyses of Growth Mindset and Other Interventions Should Follow Best Practices for Examining Heterogeneity"
- **Date:** 2023 commentary
- **PMC:** 10495100
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10495100/
- **Key findings:** Methodological commentary on the controversy between Macnamara/Burgoyne and Burnette et al. (positive findings).
- **Classroom implication:** The controversy itself is informative - effects depend heavily on which studies are included.

### 6.4 "Students' beliefs about written corrective feedback in two foreign language contexts"
- **Date:** 2025
- **Venue:** Language Awareness
- **DOI:** 10.1080/09658416.2025.2522107
- **URL:** https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09658416.2025.2522107
- **Key findings:** Students' beliefs about corrective feedback are largely independent of teachers' beliefs - and beliefs shape uptake more than feedback content.

### 6.5 "Engaging second language (L2) students with synchronous written corrective feedback"
- **Date:** 2025
- **Venue:** Humanities and Social Sciences Communications
- **DOI:** 10.1057/s41599-025-05007-3
- **URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-05007-3
- **Key findings:** Including positive comments alongside corrections substantially reduces frustration and increases revision rates. "Sandwich" technique has empirical support but only when corrective core is specific and actionable.
- **Classroom implication:** **High-leverage for samhällskunskap argumentation feedback** - frame corrective feedback within identified strengths.

## Topic 7: Feedback Timing

### 7.1 "Timing's not everything: Immediate and delayed feedback are equally beneficial for performance in formative multiple-choice testing"
- **Authors:** Ryan et al.
- **Date:** 2024
- **Venue:** Medical Education
- **DOI:** 10.1111/medu.15287
- **URL:** https://asmepublications.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/medu.15287
- **Key findings:** No significant difference between immediate and delayed feedback in formative MCQ testing. Challenges popular "immediate feedback is best" assumption.
- **Classroom implication:** Choose timing based on workflow logistics, not pedagogical superiority.

### 7.2 "Investigating the impact of immediate vs. delayed feedback timing on motivation and language learning outcomes in online education"
- **Date:** 2025
- **Venue:** System (ScienceDirect)
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.system.2025.[xxx]
- **URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0023969025000396
- **Key findings:** Immediate feedback supports motivation and emotional engagement; delayed feedback supports self-directed learning and deeper comprehension. **Different timings serve different goals** - not a single "winner."
- **Novel contribution:** Disaggregates by outcome type, dissolving the apparent contradiction.
- **Classroom implication:** Use immediate for low-stakes practice/motivation; delayed for major essays where reflective revision matters.

### 7.3 "Timing of feedback and retrieval practice: a laboratory study with EFL students"
- **Date:** 2024
- **Venue:** Humanities and Social Sciences Communications
- **DOI:** 10.1057/s41599-024-03983-6
- **URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-024-03983-6
- **Key findings:** Delayed feedback combined with retrieval practice can outperform immediate feedback for long-term retention - but the effect depends on retrieval-feedback interval matching.

### 7.4 "Prediction-error-dependent processing of immediate and delayed positive feedback"
- **Date:** 2024
- **Venue:** Scientific Reports (Nature)
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s41598-024-60328-8
- **URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-60328-8
- **Key findings:** Positive prediction errors drive feedback processing similarly for immediate and delayed feedback (in RewP and P300 ERP components). Suggests neural mechanism is timing-tolerant.

## Topic 8: Neuroscience of Feedback

### 8.1 "Dopaminergic action prediction errors serve as a value-free teaching signal"
- **Authors:** [Datta lab, Harvard]
- **Date:** 2025
- **Venue:** Nature
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s41586-025-09008-9
- **URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09008-9
- **Key findings:** Movement-related dopamine activity in tail of striatum encodes **action prediction errors** that serve as value-free teaching signals - reinforcing repeated associations independent of reward value.
- **Novel contribution:** Two distinct dopaminergic prediction-error systems exist (value-based and value-free); learning isn't only reward-driven.
- **Classroom implication:** Repetition with feedback may build skill via mechanisms separate from "rewarding" feedback - has implications for practice design.

### 8.2 "Explaining dopamine through prediction errors and beyond" (Gershman et al.)
- **Date:** 2025
- **Venue:** Annual Review-style synthesis
- **URL:** http://datta.hms.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Gershman-et-al.pdf
- **Key findings:** Updates the canonical reward prediction error theory: dopamine signals also encode features beyond value (identity, salience, sensory specifics).
- **Classroom implication:** Brain treats feedback as a multi-channel signal; surprise/novelty matter beyond just "right/wrong."

### 8.3 "Separable neural signals for reward and emotion prediction errors"
- **Date:** 2025
- **Venue:** Nature Communications
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s41467-025-63135-5
- **URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-63135-5
- **Key findings:** Affective error signals (emotional surprise) independently promote learning and have neural signatures separable from reward error signals. Emotional surprise = teaching signal in its own right.
- **Classroom implication:** Emotionally salient feedback (surprising connections, counterexamples) drives learning through a distinct mechanism - not just affect *modulating* cognitive learning.

### 8.4 "Mnemonic benefits of state curiosity - A meta-analysis"
- **Date:** 2025
- **Venue:** Psychonomic Bulletin & Review
- **DOI:** 10.3758/s13423-025-02800-8
- **URL:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-025-02800-8
- **Sample:** 47 independent studies
- **Key findings:** High curiosity states yield **g = 0.44** for task-specific recall and **g = 0.13** for incidental recall. Curiosity is a robust memory amplifier.
- **Novel contribution:** First quantitative synthesis of curiosity-memory effect across the field.
- **Classroom implication:** Feedback that opens questions ("notice the gap between X and Y - what explains it?") may be more memorable than feedback that closes them.

## Topic 9: AI/LLM Feedback in Schools (2024-2025)

### 9.1 "How does artificial intelligence compare to human feedback? A meta-analysis of performance, feedback perception, and learning dispositions"
- **Date:** 2025
- **Venue:** Educational Psychology
- **DOI:** 10.1080/01443410.2025.2553639
- **Sample:** 41 studies, 4,813 students
- **URL:** https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01443410.2025.2553639
- **Key findings:** **No statistically significant difference in learning performance** between AI-generated and human-provided feedback. AI feedback has more metacognitive suggestions and more frequent praise; human feedback rated as more credible/insightful/contextualised.
- **Novel contribution:** Best meta-analytic evidence to date that AI feedback ≈ human feedback on outcomes, though they differ qualitatively.
- **Classroom implication:** **High-leverage**: AI feedback is a viable supplement (not replacement); use it for high-volume formative cycles where human time is the bottleneck.

### 9.2 "Who Gives Feedback Matters: Student Biases Towards Human and AI-Generated Formative Feedback"
- **Authors:** Nazaretsky et al.
- **Date:** 2026 (online 2025)
- **Venue:** Journal of Computer Assisted Learning
- **DOI:** 10.1111/jcal.70153
- **URL:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jcal.70153
- **Key findings:** When feedback content is held identical, students attribute more value/effort to feedback they believe is from a human teaching assistant. Trust in AI predicts AI-attributed ratings; perceived genuineness predicts human-attributed ratings.
- **Classroom implication:** **Disclosure matters** - same AI feedback marked as "AI-generated" lands differently from "from your teacher." Ethical and pedagogical implications.

### 9.3 "Students' engagement with ChatGPT feedback: implications for student feedback literacy in the context of generative artificial intelligence"
- **Date:** 2025
- **Venue:** Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education
- **DOI:** 10.1080/02602938.2025.2471821
- **URL:** https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02602938.2025.2471821
- **Key findings:** Students engaged with ChatGPT feedback used more cognitive than metacognitive strategies; minimal emotional resistance; varying trust. Behavioral engagement was often *superficial* - quick requests, light revisions.
- **Classroom implication:** GenAI feedback ≠ automatic deeper learning; may invite "feedback shopping" without integration.

### 9.4 "AI feedback literacy in higher education: understanding, measuring, and predicting student feedback uptake"
- **Date:** 2025
- **Venue:** Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education
- **DOI:** 10.1080/02602938.2025.2587924
- **URL:** https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02602938.2025.2587924
- **Key findings:** Defines and measures **AI feedback literacy** as distinct from general feedback literacy. Predictive of uptake. Shaped by frequency of use, not demographic background.
- **Novel contribution:** Equity-relevant: AI feedback literacy is a learnable skill not predetermined by SES.
- **Classroom implication:** **Equity-positive** - structured exposure to AI feedback can close gaps rather than widen them.

### 9.5 "On Assessing the Faithfulness of LLM-generated Feedback on Student Assignments"
- **Date:** 2024 (EDM 2024)
- **Venue:** Educational Data Mining proceedings
- **URL:** https://www.educationaldatamining.org/edm2024/proceedings/2024.EDM-short-papers.49/
- **Key findings:** **>23% of LLM-generated feedback contains hallucinated content.** Both data-driven and prompt-driven systems produce hallucinations; "overpraising" is a common failure mode.
- **Classroom implication:** **Cannot use AI feedback unsupervised on substantive content**. Always verify factual claims, especially in history (dates, attributions, causal claims).

### 9.6 "Comparing Generative AI and teacher feedback: student perceptions of usefulness and trustworthiness"
- **Date:** 2025
- **Venue:** Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education
- **DOI:** 10.1080/02602938.2025.2502582
- **URL:** https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02602938.2025.2502582
- **Key findings:** Students perceive teacher feedback as more trustworthy but AI feedback as more accessible/non-judgmental. Hybrid use (AI for first-pass, teacher for final) emerges as preferred design.

### 9.7 "Can students judge like experts? A large-scale study on the pedagogical quality of AI and human personalized formative feedback"
- **Date:** 2025
- **Venue:** Computers and Education: Artificial Intelligence
- **URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666920X25001730
- **Sample:** 979 student responses, 6 STEM courses
- **Key findings:** AI and human feedback comparable on pedagogical quality; significant differences had only small effect sizes. **Both** were missing metacognitive components in >25% of cases.
- **Classroom implication:** AI doesn't underperform humans, but neither perfectly meets the metacognitive bar - both need design support.

## Topic 10: Feedback in Writing & Argumentation (samhällskunskap/historia)

### 10.1 "U.S. Secondary Students' Source-Based Argument Writing in History"
- **Authors:** Steiss, J., Wang, J., Kim, Y.-S.G., Olson, C.B.
- **Date:** 2024
- **Venue:** Written Communication (Sage)
- **DOI:** 10.1177/07410883241263549
- **Sample:** N = 207 middle and high school students
- **URL:** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/07410883241263549
- **Key findings:** Detailed dimensional analysis of source-based argument writing. Teachers received holistic scores 6 weeks post-writing; students received individualized feedback and created writing goals.
- **Empirical metrics:** Multiple writing dimensions assessed; shows where middle vs. high school students differ.
- **Classroom implication:** **HIGHLY RELEVANT for historia** - empirical map of what to look for in source-based arguments and how to give targeted formative feedback.

### 10.2 "Effects of a cognitive strategies intervention on secondary students' argument writing in history"
- **Date:** 2025
- **Venue:** Reading and Writing
- **DOI:** 10.1007/s11145-025-10678-0
- **URL:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11145-025-10678-0
- **Key findings:** Yearlong intervention combining cognitive, sociocognitive, and disciplinary approaches. Teachers in three districts received PD on supporting source-based argument writing. Improvements in writing achievement, efficacy, and strategy knowledge.

### 10.3 "Channelling feedback through audiovisual presentations: Do higher education students perceive, use and benefit from video feedback compared to written feedback?"
- **Authors:** Máñez et al.
- **Date:** 2024
- **Venue:** Journal of Computer Assisted Learning
- **DOI:** 10.1111/jcal.12993

- **Classroom implication:** **High-leverage**: short Loom-style video feedback may outperform written marginalia for essay revision.

### 10.6 "Same assignment - two different feedback contexts: lower secondary students' experiences with feedback during a three draft writing process"

## Synthesis

### Contrarian / Counter-Intuitive Findings (3-5)

1. **Growth mindset interventions show essentially null effects on achievement** in highest-quality studies (d ≈ 0.02). Despite enormous popular adoption, the empirical foundation for "you can grow" framings is weak (Macnamara & Burgoyne 2023; Gazmuri et al. 2025). **Implication:** Don't anchor feedback strategy on mindset framing; anchor on specific actionable next steps.

2. **Exemplars given AFTER first draft can outperform exemplars given before** (Nicol & Rose 2025). Conventional "model the target first" wisdom may invert the optimal order for self-regulation development.

3. **AI feedback performs as well as human feedback on learning outcomes** (meta-analysis of 41 studies, N = 4,813), but is perceived as less credible when disclosed as AI - same content, different effect (Nazaretsky et al. 2026). **Disclosure of source materially changes feedback effectiveness.**

4. **Immediate vs. delayed feedback is not a universal trade-off** - they serve different goals (motivation vs. self-directed learning) and the 2024 medical-education study found no overall difference for formative MCQ. The field's old "delayed is better" lab finding doesn't generalize to classrooms.

5. **Feedback rejection is primarily affective and relational, not cognitive** (Lipnevich et al. 2025). Students who "didn't use feedback" mostly understood it - they felt unsafe, unheard, or powerless to act. The feedback gap is a *trust* gap.

### Emerging Trends (3-5)

1. **Feedback partnerships and co-creation** replacing the deliver-receive model. Six-characteristic dialogue framework (student-initiated, adaptive, goal-aligned, non-dominating, reciprocal, cognitively engaging) is becoming standard design vocabulary.

2. **AI as a comparator/third-party enabler** rather than a replacement teacher - bridging Nicol's internal-feedback framework into the GenAI era. Pedagogically interesting hybrid designs emerging.

3. **AI feedback literacy as a distinct construct** with measurable scales (2025), and importantly shaped by *use frequency*, not demographic background - potentially equity-positive.

4. **Affect/identity moving to center stage** in feedback theory: emotion regulation, face threat, identity threat, "feedback as conversation in a relationship" rather than information transfer.

5. **Neuroscience disaggregating "feedback signals"**: action prediction errors (value-free) vs. reward prediction errors (value-based) vs. emotion prediction errors all run through separable systems. Suggests feedback design might target multiple channels deliberately.

### Research Gaps Where Evidence is Thin

1. **Nordic/Swedish-specific empirical work on feedback-literacy interventions in gymnasiet** - we have Skolverket guidance documents and a few Norwegian lower-secondary studies, but limited Swedish high-school RCTs or design-based research.

2. **Long-term effects of AI feedback on independent thinking** - all current studies are short-term (one assignment, one course). Longitudinal evidence on whether AI feedback supports or undermines metacognition over years is missing.

3. **Feedback design specifically for source-based historical argument** - Steiss et al. 2024 and Olson et al. 2025 are excellent starts but more discipline-specific work needed (counterargument feedback, contextualization feedback, corroboration feedback).

4. **Cross-cultural feedback reception in Swedish multicultural classrooms** - the Saadati 2023 review is the main reference; specific empirical work on how feedback norms interact with Swedish flerspråkig contexts is scarce.

5. **Feedback in oral assessment / classroom discussion** (relevant for samhällskunskap discussions) - heavily underrepresented compared to written feedback research.
